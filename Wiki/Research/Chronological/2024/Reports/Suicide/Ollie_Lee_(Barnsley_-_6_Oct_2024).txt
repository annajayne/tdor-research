> 'Bright, hilarious' Barnsley teen takes own life after being 'bullied to edge of existence'
> Ollie Lee took his own life aged 14 after suffering from horrible bullying
> 11:43, 27 Jan 2026
> Updated 15:55, 29 Jan 2026
> 
> The family of a Barnsley teen who took their own life after being 'bullied to the edge of his existence' has called on the government to criminalise bullying among children over the age of 10.
> 
> Willow-Tarriss Shannon Lynette Lee, known as Ollie, took his own life on October 6, 2024, after he endured relentless taunts and physical attacks, according to his mum, Courtney Lee. Ollie was a student at Barnsley Academy and accessed mental health support from Barnsley Council.
> 
> Courtney said he "died by suicide after enduring relentless bullying that was ignored, mishandled, and tragically downplayed by the systems meant to protect him".
> 
> At a pre-inquest review hearing at Sheffield Coroners' Court today (Tuesday, January 27), Assistant Coroner Hannah Berry called on witnesses representing Barnsley Council, Barnsley Academy and mental health services. The inquest has been set for May 6 to May 8 this year.

> Barnsley teenager took own life after 'relentless bullying', inquest hears
> Wednesday 6 May 2026 at 7:17pm
> A teenager who took her own life after relentless bullying had not been assessed by mental health officials despite having self-harmed in class, an inquest has heard.
> 
> Willow-Tarriss Shannon Lynette Lee, known as Ollie, died in Barnsley in October 2024 after a struggle with her mental health. She was also waiting for an assessment for autism at the time of her death.
> 
> An inquest into the 14-year-old's death opened on Wednesday, after her mum said she was subjected to relentless bullying.
> 
> The hearing at Sheffield Medico-Legal Centre also heard Willow's school - Barnsley Academy - failed to carry out risk assessments after she self-harmed twice in class in the months before her death.
> 
> Mary Potter, the school's safeguarding lead, gave evidence on Wednesday, where it emerged Ollie had suffered homophobic abuse, had been dragged over grass, sprayed with deodorant and even told to harm or kill herself.
> 
> Ollie had also used sharp objects in class to hurt herself in the months before her death, it was heard, but no safety assessments were drawn up.
> 
> She made two suicide attempts while in Year nine, returning in February 2024 after time off.
> 
> The school admitted in court it should have drawn up safety support plans, but said it believed the support provided was "appropriate”.
> 
> Describing her daughter as "an extraordinary girl who brought chaos and light into every space she entered", Ollie's mum Courtney has since called for bullying to be made a criminal offence.
> 
> Speaking on Wednesday, Ollie's father Josh said: “I clearly remember talking to someone at the council and stating that if anything else were to happen to Willow going forward I would personally hold the school and the council accountable.”
> 
> Courtney meanwhile said she supported her child "unconditionally" and had always accepted Ollie's gender identity.
> 
> “I saw a young person trying to understand themselves in a world which did not always make that easy,” she said.
> 
> Courtney claimed she raised concerns about Ollie's mental health repeatedly, and that she may never have taken her life had she been assessed by a mental health team.
> 
> The inquest, due to last until the end of the week, is also due to hear from mental health officials.
