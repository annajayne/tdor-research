> After 11 days of anguish and uncertainty, the death of Leonela Alemán Morales, a 27-year-old trans woman from Barranquilla who had been reported missing in the city of Limassol, in the Republic of Cyprus, was confirmed .
> 
> 22 years in prison for those accused of the femicide of university student Nataly Salas
> 
> The news was officially communicated to her family by the Honorary Consulate of Colombia in that city, after the authorities were able to identify the victim's body after the collapse of a building where she was staying.
> 
> The tragic incident occurred on Saturday, April 11, when a residential building suddenly collapsed. According to preliminary reports, the incident left at least two people dead and three injured.
> 
> As the days passed and after rescue and identification efforts, it was confirmed that the deceased victim was Leonela, who had recently arrived in that European country.
> 
> According to reports, the young woman had traveled as a tourist from Colombia on a flight with an initial layover in Istanbul, Turkey, before arriving in Cyprus. Since the day of the collapse, there had been no information about her whereabouts, which caused concern among her family and friends.
> 
> Amid the uncertainty, the well-known LGBT influencer known as 'La Ricarda', originally from the municipality of San Jacinto, Bolívar, brought the case to light on social media, requesting support in locating him. The outreach allowed more people to learn of the situation and join the search.
> 
> Local media in Cyprus have indicated that the collapsed building had already been listed as uninhabitable, raising questions about the conditions and potential liability.
> 
> Costas Michael, director of the Limassol Housing Authority, indicated that the property had 11 apartments and that approximately 20 people resided there, Euronews revealed .
> 
> It has been learned that Leonela Alemán Morales grew up in the San Nicolás neighborhood of Barranquilla, and her death has plunged her entire family into mourning. Cypriot authorities are continuing to investigate the causes of the building collapse to clarify this tragic event.




> After more than a week of uncertainty, on the morning of Tuesday, April 21, the death of Leonela Alemán Morales , 27, from Barranquilla , was confirmed. She had been reported missing after the collapse of a building in the city of Limassol, Cyprus , on April 11.
> 
> The information was confirmed by consular officials to the young woman's family after identity verifications were carried out in the European country. This was announced by Johana Alemán, Leonela's sister , who stated that she received the official call from the consul on Tuesday morning.
> 
> “A while ago, about an hour ago, the consul called me and informed me that my sister is one of the deceased , but that we urgently need my mother to take a DNA test ,” Johana told the local newspaper El Heraldo, explaining that this procedure is essential to continue with the corresponding legal proceedings .
> 
> In official documents such as her passport, the victim is listed under the name Leonardo Alemán Morales . However, her family confirmed that Leonela was a transgender woman , a member of the LGBTIQ+ community in Barranquilla.
> 
> According to European media reports, the incident occurred at midday on Saturday, April 11 , shortly before 1:30 p.m., when the side of a two-story building , consisting of nine apartments, collapsed in the Germosogeia sector , a residential area in the municipality of Limassol . The building was being used as accommodation for foreign nationals who were temporarily in the country.
> 
> Following the collapse, local authorities launched search and rescue operations in the rubble, while protocols were activated to identify those affected . From that same day, Leonela's family lost all contact with her, triggering alerts and initiating a search from Colombia .
> 
> Johana recounted that her last communication with her sister was before the accident , and that, after receiving no response for several hours, she began contacting friends and acquaintances, without success. Later, she learned of the building collapse through reports in international media , which led her to make contact with consulates and diplomatic authorities .
> 
> During the weekend prior to the official confirmation, the family maintained communications with various consular bodies , until they were finally informed that Leonela was among the fatalities of the accident, awaiting definitive confirmation through genetic identification tests .
> More procedures are needed
> 
> However, the process now faces an additional obstacle . To proceed with the repatriation of the body to Colombia , authorities require a DNA test , which must be performed on a direct relative, in this case, the young woman's mother . According to the family, the testing facility in Barranquilla is currently unavailable , which has delayed the procedures .
> 
> “We’ve been in contact with Interpol, but they tell us they don’t have the proper facilities here to conduct the test . Even so, they recommended we go to the Forensic Medicine Institute ,” explained Johana, who expressed her concern about the possibility of having to travel to another country to complete the required procedure.
> 
> The family says they lack the financial resources to cover travel or additional expenses outside the city. “ My mother doesn't have the means, and neither do we, to get around. We're knocking on doors to see who can help us , because we can't leave her there ,” she stated.
> 
> According to her family, Leonela left Barranquilla for Bogotá on Tuesday, March 31, where she spent the night. Later, on April 1, she boarded a flight to Istanbul , from where she continued her journey to Cyprus . The young woman had stated that her trip to Europe was for tourism purposes .
> 
> According to reports, her plans included visiting other countries on the continent , including France , before returning to Barranquilla in mid-May . Leonela was single and maintained an active presence on social media , where she shared aspects of her daily life.
> 
> While still processing the news of his death , his family has indicated they will seek the cooperation of national authorities to facilitate the identification and repatriation of his body . For now, the process remains on hold pending the completion of the genetic testing required by foreign authorities.
> 
> The death of Leonela Alemán Morales has caused profound shock among her fellow citizens who recently learned of the news, while her family in Barranquilla faces grief and the administrative hurdles inherent in a death abroad . May she rest in peace.



> A 27-year-old woman from Colombia has been identified as the second victim found in the rubble of a building that collapsed in Limassol’s Yermasoyia on April 11.
> 
> Colombian press named the victim as Leonella Aleman Morales, who had been declared missing by her family after they lost contact with her.
> 
> The last ping from Morales’ mobile phone had been pinpointed at the collapsed building.
> 
> The family was informed of her death by the Colombian honorary consulate in Cyprus following the identification of her body.
> 
> The other victim is 27-year-old Dex from Nigeria.
> 
> The building that partially collapsed had already been declared dangerous in March, with owners warned to evacuate and carry out repairs, according to the president of the Limassol district government (EOA) Yiannis Tsouloftas.
> 
> Tsouloftas said the property had been inspected on February 19 and was officially classified as dangerous on March 10.



> December 31, 2025, was the last time Leonela Alemán Morales gathered with her family on the terrace of her home in the San Nicolás neighborhood, in southern Barranquilla. The New Year's celebration was their final opportunity together before she traveled to Cyprus, where she tragically lost her life in the collapse of the building where she was staying in the city of Limassol on April 11.
> 
> A few days before that last large family gathering, on December 23, she surprised her mother, Luz Mariana Morales, with a surprise visit. Her two other daughters, Johana and Angélica, had been giving her clues throughout the day. “She was very special to me. On December 23rd, when she arrived, I didn't know; it was a surprise. Then, my eldest daughter asked me, ‘Who is the love of your life?’ I asked her, ‘Why?’ I said Leonela; then, when she arrived later, she told me, ‘Mommy, the love of your life has arrived,’ and we were able to celebrate with her,” she recalled in an interview with EL HERALDO, her grief palpable, sitting in her living room.
> 
> Her eyes immediately filled with tears when she remembered that Leonela had stayed until her birthday, just a few weeks prior, and that after that she traveled to Europe: “On my birthday, we were here because her sister had just given birth and the baby was a little sick. She said that, since we couldn't go anywhere, she was going to have a get-together here. And she was so happy to be with me.”
> 
> However, fate dealt her the most bitter of surprises. In just a couple of weeks, he went from promising to take her to France to desperately searching for her until he received a call from the Colombian consul in Cyprus confirming that Leonela was one of the fatalities in the collapse of the 11-apartment building.
> 
> “We last spoke on April 7th. After that, we never heard from each other again. That day she told me she was going to Israel, then on to France, and then coming home to spend her birthday with me on July 20th,” he lamented.
