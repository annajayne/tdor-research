> Vica Monteros, a 44-year-old transgender architect and prominent figure in the LGBTQ+ community , was murdered in her home in the Alberdi neighborhood of Córdoba, Argentina . The case is being investigated by provincial authorities as a homicide. The crime came to light after a fire broke out in the house, and a suspect has been arrested while forensic examinations and witness testimonies continue.
> 
> The discovery came after neighbors noticed smoke coming from a property on Mendoza Street in the 300 block and alerted the authorities. When police entered, they found the body with multiple stab wounds and signs of extreme violence . Reports also indicate that the fire may have been started to cover up the attack and that open gas valves were found, suggesting an attempt to cause an explosion.
> 
> The investigation was led by prosecutor Guillermo González, who stated publicly that the evidence gathered points to a premeditated murder and that, after analyzing security camera footage, taking testimonies, and conducting raids, an arrest was made. The suspect is a 27-year-old man, and authorities indicated that he will be transferred to the Bouwer prison while his legal status is determined.
> 
> Beyond the legal proceedings, Vica Monteros's name became symbolic for what she represented in Córdoba. Born in Tucumán, she had settled in the city of Córdoba to pursue her career as an architect , and was recognized both for her work and for her involvement in community spaces related to diversity. In this dual journey—profession and activism— she had become a familiar figure to many people who described her as someone dedicated to her projects and to supporting others.
> 
> One of her most frequently mentioned legacies, according to those who mourned her, was " Fuma Espuma ," the inclusive soccer team she helped to establish and which was recognized as the first gay soccer team in Córdoba . For those close to her, it wasn't just about playing: it was a space of belonging, visibility, and support for a community that has historically faced barriers in sports and everyday life. After her murder, messages of grief and demands for justice were shared from that organization.
> 
> The impact of the crime was also felt in his home province. Some of his family members began making arrangements to travel to Córdoba to assist with the necessary procedures, while friends and family circulated requests for help to cover expenses related to the transfer and funeral.
> 
> Meanwhile, LGBTQ+ organizations and leaders publicly raised the need for the justice system to consider whether the attack could be classified as a hate crime or transfemicide , a discussion that often arises when the brutality and context of violence are combined with a history of structural discrimination. So far, this possibility has not been ruled out in the public sphere, and the debate continues alongside the investigation, although the case is currently focused on establishing the sequence of events, the suspect's relationship with the perpetrator, and the motive.
> 
> While the prosecution continues with expert analyses (autopsy, traces at the scene, reconstruction of movements by cameras and devices) and taking statements, the case of Vica Monteros left one certainty outside the courts: her name is no longer mentioned only for the brutal way in which she died, but for what she did in life to open spaces of belonging .






> Vica Monteros , a 44-year-old trans architect and a leading figure in the LGBTIQ + community of Córdoba , was murdered with 37 stab wounds in her home in the Alberdi neighborhood . 
> 
> Police arrested a 27-year-old man on Wednesday, identified as  Maximiliano "Vasco" Sallito , who is now behind bars as the main suspect.
> 
> The victim's family, originally from Tucumán, was emphatic: "It's a hate crime ," attributing the attack to his gender identity. Furthermore, Monteros had founded Córdoba's first gay soccer team years earlier .
> 
> According to preliminary investigations, the two had arranged to meet virtually at the victim's home. After committing the crime, the assailant allegedly turned on the gas and set the house on fire to conceal the crime, before fleeing with valuables.
> 
> Residents of Mendoza Street noticed the smoke, knocked on the door, and when there was no answer, broke it down. Once inside, they managed to extinguish the fire and found Monteros's body.
> 
> The first trace of the suspect emerged from conversations found on the victim's computer . The prosecution analyzed security camera footage and traced the route of the Uber that Sallito had hired hours before the crime.
> 
> Prosecutor Guillermo González , of District Attorney's Office 1, Shift 2, charged Sallito with "homicide aggravated by cruelty and treachery" .
> 
> The body showed bruising and multiple stab wounds. According to investigators, "the killer wasn't satisfied with seeing the lifeless body, but continued attacking until the knife broke . "
