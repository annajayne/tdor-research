> July 1, 2026
> Nikole, a trans woman, was murdered in Villavicencio during Pride Day. 
> 
> On June 28, while the activities commemorating Pride Day for sexual and gender diversity were concluding in the streets, a trans woman known as Nikole Torres was murdered in Villavicencio, Meta.
> 
> The Metropolitan Police confirmed the discovery of his body around 5:50 p.m. in the Villa Suárez neighborhood. According to authorities, the victim had a gunshot wound to the neck. The case remains under investigation.
> 
> The Mayor's Office of Villavicencio, through its Office of Gender and Diversity (OSIGD), condemned the murder and expressed its "deepest rejection and sorrow at the persistent acts of violence that violate the life and dignity" of the LGBTIQ+ population. In the statement, the administration lamented Nicole's murder, noting that the incident "raises alarms about the urgent need to protect those facing the greatest vulnerability" and highlighting that the victim was homeless, which demonstrates "the severity of the intersectional violence suffered by trans women in contexts of extreme exclusion."
> 
> For its part, the Villavicencio Critical Pride collective, through a manifesto, linked Nikole's murder to the structural violence faced by trans people in Colombia. In the document, the organization maintains that the case "is not isolated" and demands a swift investigation and guaranteed justice. Furthermore, it denounces the persistence of institutional barriers, social exclusion, and the invisibility of violence against people with diverse sexual orientations and gender identities. 
> 
> The manifesto points out that Villavicencio continues to be a place where LGBTIQ+ people face discrimination, violence, and insufficient institutional support. In particular, it warns that trans women experience greater vulnerability due to exclusion from education, employment, the economy, and the healthcare system—factors that, according to the group, increase their exposure to lethal violence. 
> As Critical Pride points out, Nikole's murder occurs within a context of persistent violence against trans women in Colombia. According to the Ombudsman's Office, based on figures from the Attorney General's Office, the country registered 30 trans femicides in 2025, one more than the previous year. This figure coincides with the record of the organization Caribe Afirmativo, which also documented 12 trans femicides during the first months of 2026.
