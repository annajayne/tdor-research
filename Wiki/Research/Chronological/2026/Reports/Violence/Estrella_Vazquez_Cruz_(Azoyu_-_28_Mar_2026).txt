> Transgender woman shot dead in the Costa Chica region of Guerrero
> With the murder of Estrella, the number of crimes against LGBTI+ people in Guerrero has reached 53 in the last 10 years, according to collectives.
> 
> CHILPANCINGO, Gro., (apro) .- Estrella Vázquez, a transgender woman, was found shot to death over the weekend in Azoyú, a municipality in the Costa Chica region of Guerrero.
> 
> With this, the number of crimes against LGBTI+ people in Guerrero has reached 53 in the last 10 years, according to collectives.
> 
> “They are killing us simply for belonging to the LGBT community,” they warned.
> 
> Yesterday, the murder of Estrella Vázquez Cruz, 36, from the community of Macahuite, was reported. She had disappeared on Thursday, March 26.
> 
> Her body was found on Saturday by residents about five kilometers from her community, on a plot of land near Azoyú, the municipal seat of the same name.
> 
> At the crime scene, authorities found at least two spent shell casings from a long gun.
> 
> This Tuesday, the newly created Guerrero Diverse State Front, made up of 15 collectives, condemned the murder and demanded that authorities stop the attacks against the community.
> 
> He denounced that in 2026, two more hate crimes were committed against Ares Navarrete, on February 8 in Chilpancingo, and against José Alberto Gálvez, on March 14 in Marquelia.
> 
> For its part, the Orgullo Guerrero collective reported that the National Observatory of Hate Crimes against LGBTI People in Mexico has a record of 50 crimes against members of this community between 2016 and 2025, plus the three that occurred in 2026.
> 
> “We require official information from the authorities regarding these crimes and investigations conducted with a gender perspective and without prejudice, so that there is no impunity,” said members of the organization.
> 
> When consulted by Apro, the president of Orgullo Guerrero, Efraín de Jesús Arroyo, lamented that these crimes continue to be committed in Guerrero on the International Transgender Day of Visibility.
> 
> He denounced the underreporting of crimes in the state because those crimes are not made public in municipalities in the Central, Northern, and Mountain regions.
> 
> He demanded justice from the authorities for these murders because investigations are opened, but there is no follow-up on attacks against the LGBTI population.
> 
> Furthermore, the state law does not specifically define hate crimes motivated by homophobia and transphobia.
> 
> He stated that machismo, ideas, and ways of thinking that attack sexual minorities prevail in all social strata.
> 
> "They are killing us simply for belonging to the LGBTI community," the activist declared.
