> Trans-femicide in Mexico City: JUSTICE FOR IVONNE
> 
> Ivonne, a 32-year-old trans woman, was tortured and murdered at her beauty salon in Iztapalapa. One person has been arrested.
> 
> On July 1st, Ivonne Álvarez, a 32-year-old trans woman, was tortured and murdered at her beauty salon in Iztapalapa, a borough located in the eastern part of Mexico City. According to information from the Mexico City Attorney General's Office, her murder is being investigated under the femicide protocol, and a man allegedly involved in the crime has been arrested following the investigation.
> 
> In an interview with *Presentes*, Silvia, Ivonne’s sister, speaks candidly: "I feel terrible. I can't believe this because she went out to work; she was supposed to come back. I used to wait for her just like every other day—waiting for her to arrive in her van with the music blasting and honking the horn. I still think she’s going to come back, that I’ll see her getting ready, putting on her wig, and asking me, 'How do I look?' And I’d tell her, 'Beautiful.' Because I always saw her as beautiful. But the truth is, I have to accept that she won't—I won't see her anymore."
> 
> Ivonne was originally from Nezahualcóyotl (State of Mexico) but worked 13 kilometers away in Iztapalapa (Mexico City). She enjoyed having tacos and smoothies for dinner, dancing, and listening to electro-house and pop music at full volume. In March of this year, she opened her own business: a beauty salon and barbershop.
> 
> To open her salon, Ivonne had to sell her car and engage in sex work. "There were few clients because of the situation, you know? So, to have money and be able to eat, she also sold desserts and worked as a sex worker—simply because she didn't make enough money otherwise—but she opened the salon so she could stop doing that [sex work]," Silvia explains.
> 
> She was murdered in her salon.
> 
> At 9:00 PM on Wednesday, June 30th, family and friends lost contact with Ivonne. “She was the kind of person who was on the phone all day—constantly talking to her friends or to me; we always knew where she was, you know? But suddenly, her calls started going straight to voicemail,” Silvia recounts in an interview.
> 
> On the morning of July 1st, Silvia went to Ivonne’s house but found neither her nor her SUV. Finding this strange, Silvia contacted a couple of Ivonne’s friends to see if they had spotted her at her beauty salon. “One of them told me she saw her go into the salon with a young man, and that when she came back, the security shutter [a metal mesh gate] was down and her SUV was gone.”
> 
> Alarmed by this information, Silvia decided to go look for her sister. Her calls continued to go straight to voicemail.
> 
> While traveling from Neza to Iztapalapa, Silvia received a call from one of Ivonne’s friends who noticed something “odd” at the salon: the security shutter was locked with only two of its three padlocks. “Her friend called to say she was going to peek through the small window—she had to borrow a ladder to do so—and told me, ‘I can see someone wearing green in there.’” Silvia knew Ivonne had been wearing green when she had last asked about her.
> 
> When Silvia arrived at the salon, police were already on the scene; in her anguish, she demanded to see Ivonne. “I saw her—I saw her with her hands and feet tied, bound with tape, and a cleaning cloth wrapped around her head. The cause of death we were given was asphyxiation by strangulation,” she recounts.
> 
> So far this year, 16 trans women have been murdered in Mexico, according to the National Observatory of Hate Crimes Against LGBT People.
> 
> “We want justice, and nothing else.”
> 
> Police located Ivonne’s SUV ten minutes away from her salon. A man and a woman were inside the vehicle. In an interview, Silvia recounts: “When the police arrived, he confessed everything; the police didn’t even have to ask. He just blurted out, ‘I fucked up, I fucked up,’ and said he had done it with a friend.”
> 
> Silvia is convinced that at least one other person remains at large, as she believes a single individual could not have inflicted the full extent of the violence Ivonne suffered.
> 
> On July 5, the Mexico City Prosecutor’s Office issued a statement announcing that detectives from the Investigative Police—attached to the Office of the Prosecutor for the Investigation of Femicide—had arrested a man who was allegedly with Ivonne at the beauty salon on the night she was murdered. The detained individual is currently in the custody of a supervisory judge at the Eastern Men’s Preventive Prison.
> 
> “We want nothing more than justice for my sister, because those men shouldn't be allowed to walk free as if nothing happened; if they did that to her, what else might they do to others—to other women? We want justice, and nothing else,” Silvia asserts.
> 
> Mexico does not legally recognize the specific crime of transfemicide, nor has the State made efforts to assess the specific experiences and needs of trans populations in the country. In the view of Rocío Suárez—a human rights defender and member of the Center for Support of Trans Identities (CAIT)—this remains “one of the major outstanding issues...”
