> In the early hours of Thursday, September 17, the murder of Roxxy Vanessa Astaíza, a trans social leader and LGBTIQ+ activist, who was recognized for her work as a human rights defender, was confirmed.
> 
> The young woman was murdered in Popayán, Cauca, with a bladed weapon. The incident reportedly occurred during a fight.
> 
> Despite being taken to a hospital, she died minutes later due to the severity of her injuries.
> 
> Astaiza was a renowned LGBTIQ+ leader and member of the Mariposas Negras collective, an organization of trans women that develops processes of defending rights and fighting against violence in the department of Cauca.
> 
> Her work had made her a respected figure within activism for the rights of sexual and gender minorities in the region.
> 
> According to Indepaz, this is the 111th social leader to be murdered so far in 2026.
> 
> The activist was a candidate for the Cauca Departmental Advisory Council for Women for the period 2026-2029, an electoral process currently being carried out by the Cauca Governorate.
> 
> Their political participation represented a significant step forward in the inclusion of diverse voices in regional decision-making spaces.
> 
> Authorities indicated that the events are under investigation and that a possible hypothesis is a suspected transfemicide.
> 
> The alleged perpetrator of the crime was captured.
> 
> However, there are no further details about his identity or the motive for the murder.




> Aida Quilcué denounces the murder of a trans leader and candidate for the Cauca Women's Advisory Council: “Life and dignity must be protected”
> The member of *Mariposas Negras* (Black Butterflies) died after sustaining injuries in Popayán; Indepaz has called for an investigation into whether the crime was motivated by her gender identity.
> September 17, 2026, 1:37 PM EST
>
> Roxxy Vanessa Astaiza, an LGBTIQA+ leader and member of the *Mariposas Negras* collective, died on September 17, 2026, in Popayán, Cauca, after being wounded in an incident currently under investigation by authorities. The Institute for Development and Peace Studies (Indepaz) warned that the case could constitute an alleged transfemicide.
> 
> Gustavo Petro responded to Luis Felipe Henao, the acting president of Ecopetrol—who claimed the previous administration left the country on the brink of a blackout—by stating: “We left behind trillions of pesos.”
> See also: Gustavo Petro responded to Luis Felipe Henao, the acting president of Ecopetrol, who claimed the previous administration left the country on the brink of a blackout: “We left behind trillions of pesos.”
> Astaiza was a member of *Mariposas Negras*, an organization of trans women that advocates for rights and takes action against violence. She was also a candidate for the Cauca Departmental Women's Advisory Council for the 2026–2029 term, as part of the electoral process being conducted by the Cauca Governor's Office. > 
> According to information released by Indepaz, the activist was wounded during what was initially described as an altercation. Following the attack, she was taken to a medical facility, where she died due to the severity of her injuries.
> 
> The institute included the crime in its registry of attacks against social leaders. With Astaiza's death, the organization recorded 111 leaders and human rights defenders murdered in Colombia during 2026.
> 
> Roxxy Vanessa Astaiza's death coincides with warnings from the Ombudsman's Office regarding risk factors for the civilian population in Popayán. The agency issued Early Warning 018 of 2026, which identifies distinct threats in both rural and urban sectors of the Cauca capital.
> 
> Ex-partner of the alleged killer of young Ana Lucía González—who had been reported missing in Mosquera—speaks out: "I was suspicious of him."
> 
> In rural areas, the document notes an increased presence and activity of armed groups, with disputes linked to the control of territory and transit corridors. Within the city, the warning notes that various criminal structures use the municipality to seek refuge, obtain resources, and expand their economic and political influence.
> 
> The Ombudsman's Office issued 27 recommendations aimed at strengthening institutional prevention and response measures. The goal is to reduce the exposure of communities and social organizations to acts of violence.
> 
> Indepaz identified the armed groups present in the area as including the National Liberation Army (ELN); the "Comandante Jacobo Arenas" Western Bloc—which comprises the Jaime Martínez, Dagoberto Ramos, and Carlos Patiño fronts—as well as "Los Comapan" and other local gangs.
> 
> Aida Quilcué called for the circumstances of the crime to be clarified.
> 
> Aida Quilcué, a representative of the *Pacto Histórico* (Historic Pact), urged the Attorney General's Office to move quickly to clarify the murder of Roxxy Vanessa Astaiza Mosquera—a trans woman, philosophy student, and prominent figure in the "Trans Las Mariposas Negras" collective.
> 
> Aida Quilcué urged the Attorney General's Office to expedite the investigation into Roxxy Vanessa Astaiza's murder – credit: @aida_quilcue/X
> 
> The congresswoman posted a message on her X account expressing her condemnation of the activist's death and demanding guarantees for trans women in the country.
> 
> "We condemn the murder," wrote Quilcué regarding Astaiza, who had also sought to join the Departmental Women's Advisory Council. The activist was participating in the electoral process to form that body within the department.
