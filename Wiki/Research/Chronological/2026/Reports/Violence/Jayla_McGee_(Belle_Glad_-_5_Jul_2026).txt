> Black trans teen Jayla McGee died a violent death on July 5th in Florida. Why isn’t the media covering her story?
> July 23, 2026 by Sue
> 
> Screenshot
> Another Black trans teen appears to have been killed, this time near Belle Glade, Florida. Belle Glade is part of Palm Beach. Her name was Jayla McGee and she was 19.
> 
> Reports surfaced on Facebook and other social media platforms that a 19-year-old Black trans person had died a violent death on Sunday, July 5, 2026. There are no media reports specifically related to this reported death. Comments suggest Jayla was shot to death at close range.
> 
> I’m a little shocked that we cannot find any information, any acknowledgment that this young person was ripped from this mortal coil by gunshot. There are many reports of violent incidents over that time period, but none match up with the facts that Jayla’s friends are posting.
> 
> While there is no public obituary at this time, the family has announced a viewing Friday night followed by a celebration of life on Saturday. Colors are pink and white.
> 
> Jayla also used the names Javarous (perhaps assigned name?) and Baby J. According to one friend, Jayla had begun hormone replacement therapy and was planning her top surgery just days before her death. Some of the reel clips reference LGBTQ+ and ballroom community affiliations.
> 
> Jayla was born and raised in Belle Glade, Florida. She had a big beautiful family of siblings, cousins, aunts and uncles and more whose grief has demonstrated the deep love for this young woman. Coupled with disbelief that she was killed, their voices and her story should be acknowledged.
> 
> A prolific user of social media, Jayla filled the feeds with funny videos, memes, proud photos of her loved ones, and lots of back and forth with her friends.
> 
> “I never had to question if you really loved me because I knew you did you appreciated me and I appreciated and accepted you for who you were/”
> 
> “You will forever be missed you most definitely was the life of the party”
> 
> Friends organized a vigil on July 18, 2026 – the video has been shared many times.
> 
> What struck me is that as much as she used social media, as open and vivid her life appeared, she really kept many things private. I don’t [know] where she went to school, if she had a job, what her dreams were, what her favorites things were. But she did share her transition or rather her gender identity moving deftly between names, pronouns, and profiles, a sort of social fluidity that perhaps someone like myself, a cis woman, simply cannot understand.
> 
> I do understand that a lot of people loved Jayla in all her incarnations and with all of her realness, many people are just devastated.
> 
> It remains hard to understand how someone so young and vivacious and popular could be killed over a holiday weekend with nary a word in mainstream media. Her life and her death matter. She should be acknowledged. She should not be dead, she should not have been shot, and she she should not be ignored.
> 
> Jayla’s family experienced a similar tragedy in 2022 when her then 8-year-old sister Ronziyah was shot to death in the family’s front yard. That’s a lot of pain for any family to endure. Jayla would have been 14 at time time, a tender age to experience such a loss.
> 
> That additional loss makes the media silence even more perplexing. Perhaps Jayla’s death was not violent and she does not belong on the list I curate, but her life and death absolutely put her on the larger lists – to honor and commemorate her life, to always remember her death.
