> Bangladeshi transgender community leader found dead as man arrested
> Since her death, police have arrested 34-year-old Md Momin and suspect robbery may have been the motive
> By Aaron Sugg
> 
> Bangladeshi hijra community leader Mohammad Mohsin, also known as Mousumi Hijra and ‘Guruma’, was found dead at her home in Chattogram on Saturday (5 September).
> 
> Police recovered the 52-year-old’s body from her home in the hijra colony near Ambagan Railway Colony at around 10:30am. She was reportedly found lying face down on her bed, with marks around her neck.

> Police initially suspected she had been strangled and launched a murder investigation. Her body was taken to Chattogram Medical College Hospital for a post-mortem examination.
> 
> Police attended the scene after community members became concerned when she did not respond. One of her associates later found her dead.
> 
> Following her death, members of the hijra community gathered outside her residence and called for a fair investigation.
> 
> According to Newage, police have arrested 34-year-old suspect Md Momin, also known as Sheikh Roni, after CCTV footage allegedly showed him entering her room in the early hours of the day she died.
> 
> Momin reportedly entered Moushumi’s room at 1:14am on Saturday wearing a white T-shirt and left at 3:52am, after spending nearly three hours inside.
> 
> Police identified Momin through CCTV footage and arrested him in the Pahartali area of Chattogram on Monday (7 September). Police say he confessed during preliminary questioning to his involvement in the killing.
> 
> They allege that Mousumi had called Momin to her home saying she would give him money. According to police, the pair later argued over money and a sexual relationship, before Momin allegedly strangled her with a nylon strap and fled.
> 
> Money and two mobile phones were also reportedly missing from the property, and police had initially considered robbery as a possible motive.
> 
> The exact circumstances and motive remain under investigation.



> A transgender community leader known as Mousumi was allegedly killed by extremists inside her home in Bangladesh’s southeastern city of Chattogram on Saturday, transgender community members said, amid growing fears of violence against the marginalised group.
> 
> Mousumi, a guru ma — a senior figure in the local transgender community — was found dead at about 10 a.m. inside her home in Masud Colony, near the railway line in the city’s Ambagan area, members of the community said.
> 
> Witnesses said her body bore a thin mark around the neck, while blood was visible around her mouth and her tongue was protruding. Community members also said they saw injuries to her neck and chest.
> 
> Khulshi police chief Abdur Rahim said officers were investigating the circumstances surrounding the death.
> 
> “We are investigating who killed a member of the transgender community,” he said.
> 
> Police sent the body to Chattogram Medical College Hospital for a post-mortem examination.
> 
> Pakhi, a member of the transgender community who entered Mousumi’s room, said the scene immediately raised alarm.
> 
> “Around 10 a.m., I entered the room and saw blood coming from her mouth. Her tongue was sticking out,” Pakhi said. “We called the people around us and then informed the police.”
> 
> Mousumi’s death prompted grief and anger among transgender people in the neighbourhood, many of whom described her as a respected community leader who routinely helped people in distress.
> 
> A community member who requested anonymity alleged that Mousumi was “brutally killed by extremists” after attackers entered her home.
> 
> “Mousumi was very popular in the area. Whenever anyone was in trouble, she would come forward to help,” the person said. “We cannot understand who could have been her enemy. We want justice for our loved one.”
> 
> The person added: “We believe transgender people are increasingly being targeted by extremists and hostile elements. We are afraid. We want justice for our loved one.”
> 
> Another community member said the killing had shattered a sense of security among transgender residents.
> 
> “Our people are already facing discrimination, harassment and violence. Now we are afraid even inside our own homes,” the person said. “If Mousumi was deliberately targeted, the killers must be identified and punished.”
> 
> Her death has brought back memories of the brutal killing of Shila, a transgender activist in Rangamati, in February 2025.
> 
> The transgender community has also been shaken by a separate incident in Narsingdi on 24 May 2026, when two transgender people were reportedly stabbed. One died and another was injured.
> 
> A transgender community member said the situation had become more difficult since the fall of the government led by former prime minister Sheikh Hasina in August 2024.
> 
> “There is a breakdown in law and order in Bangladesh, and we are frightened. We need protection, not sympathy after someone is killed,” the community member said.



> Transgender community leader found dead in Chattogram
> Staff Correspondent. Chattogram  05 September, 2026, 18:42
> A leader of the transgender community was found dead at her home in the Ambagan Railway Colony area of Khulshi in Chattogram on Saturday morning
> 
> The deceased was identified as Mohammad Mohsin alias Mousumi, 52, who was known as a ‘guruma’ among a group of transgender people living in Chattogram.
> 
> Police recovered her body from her residence adjacent to the hijra colony in Ambagan Railway Colony at about 10:00am.
> 
> Mousumi lived alone in the house, locals said.
> 
> According to local people, Mousumi had been chatting with her neighbours until about 2:00am before going to sleep. As she did not wake up by 10:00am, neighbours went to check on her and found her lying motionless inside the house.
> 
> Khulshi police sub-inspector Golam Mohammad Nasim said neighbours found the door of Mousumi’s house open in the morning.
> 
> ‘Her body was found lying face down on the bed in the bedroom. Blood was coming from her mouth and there were serious injury marks on her throat,’ he said.
> 
> The body was sent to Chattogram Medical College Hospital for an autopsy.
> 
> The actual cause of death would be known after the autopsy report is received, SI Nasim said.
