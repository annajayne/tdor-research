> Black Trans Woman Aleanna Royal Bechdel Fatally Stabbed in New York State
> April 22, 2026 by Sue
> 
> Her name was Aleanna Belcher. She was a 31 year old Black trans woman when her life ended on Friday, April 17, 2026 in the stairwell of a housing complex in Birmingham, New York. She knew the man who stabbed her to death, but the nature of their relationship is unclear.
> 
> Aleanna Belcher, a 31-year-old from Johnson City, died at the hospital after suffering a significant stab wound to the abdomen at a housing facility on Virgil Street on Friday, according to information from the Binghamton Police Department. Belcher was aided at the scene by police before EMS arrived and took Belcher to the hospital, where they later died.
> 
> Police have made an arrest and filed second degree murder charges. This remains an active investigation, and anyone with information is asked to call the Binghamton police Detective Bureau at 607-772-7080.
> 
> As we have seen with other recent situations, the media deadnamed and misgendered her. So once again her friends and family have to add making those corrections to their grieving process. I suspect we are sliding backwards on this reporting.
> 
> Honoring Aleanna
> She was born in Las Vegas. Aleanna was known as Aleanna Royal and Queen Freakii. She studied at the University of Nevada Las Vegas. Aleanna identified as a dj, promoter, dancer, model, artist and more.
> 
> Her social show her fondness for music and dance. I noticed that she ‘liked’ many small businesses and artists – showing up for them in that small, but meaningful way.
> 
> Post reads Justice for Freaki with a trans pride flag, candles and gold and purple accents.
> Friends say “She Lived and loved the RAVE. It was her thang. She loved dubstep, and bass and drum n bass and house music.”
> 
> “She was an amazing person. Always positive and happy. She LOVED the edm scene and loved to dance.”
> 
> Family have set up a crowdfund to help with her final expenses – they are just $500 short of the goal. Her brother wrote
> 
> “Aleanna was an artist, loved drawing, loved to dance, and could light up a room just by entering.
> 
> More than anything, Aleanna was the best at bringing people together, and was loved by many.
> 
> Aleanna was known for helping in the community, was very good with kids, and “Aunty Aleena” was the life of the party, always bringing positive vibes.
> 
> Aleanna stuck up for people, and never liked to see anyone down—a natural protector with a great soul.”

> Her funeral is planned for Saturday in Nevada. Sadly the church where she grew up and her family attended withdrew the offer to hold her funeral service after, according to her brother, they learned she was trans.
