> "It was either me or them," says man who killed trans woman and her husband in testimony.
> The crime occurred on Friday; Deivison was released at a custody hearing.
> By Clara Farias | 07/06/2026 13:16
> 
> "It was either me, or it was them," said Deivison Felipe Alves de Brito, 30, during questioning about the death of trans woman Natália dos Anjos Molina, 33, and her husband, Ademar Spacino Júnior, 38. The crime took place in the early hours of Friday (5), in Campo Grande. Arrested in flagrante after the murders, he was released at a custody hearing this Saturday (6), a decision that prompted an appeal from the Public Prosecutor's Office of Mato Grosso do Sul.
> 
> During his testimony, Deivison claimed to have acted in self-defense. According to him, the altercation began after an argument involving his wife and the couple. He stated that Natália had attacked his wife and that, faced with the situation, he grabbed his firearm and fired the first shots from the gate of the residence.
> 
> Deivison said he took the unloaded gun from the top of the cupboard, loaded the bullets, and then reloaded it. According to him, the first shot was fired into the floor to scare Natália. "He didn't stop. Then I shot him again, I think four times. Then I shot him again, managed to load another two bullets, and continued," he stated.
> 
> According to Deivison, Natália's husband asked what was happening and was called by his wife to come to the location. The accused stated that Ademar went towards the kitchen to get a knife and then approached him. "I fired one to scare him, he didn't stop [...] I fired a couple more shots to see if he would stop, and he kept running into his house," he said.
> 
> In an interview, Natália's mother reported that her daughter and husband were frequently threatened by Deivison and his wife, and that Natália was killed inside their home. "It was a hate crime, just because she's a trans woman, it was definitely hate. [...] I lost my daughter, a life like that makes no sense to me," she told reporters on the day of the crime.
> 
> Following Deivison's release at a custody hearing, the MPMS (Public Prosecutor's Office of Mato Grosso do Sul) appealed the decision and requested the Court of Justice to order his preventive detention. In the appeal, the agency argues that there is strong evidence of authorship, including the suspect's confession, the seizure of the weapon used, and expert evidence indicating multiple shots fired at the victims. The MPMS also highlights that Natália was hit by three shots in the back and cites the possibility that the crime was motivated by discrimination, since one of the victims was a transgender woman.
> 
> The Public Prosecutor's Office further argues that the suspect's freedom poses a risk to public order, potentially compromising the criminal investigation, influencing witnesses, and generating insecurity among the victims' families. According to the request, the analysis of potential self-defense requires further investigation and cannot be concluded prematurely during the custody hearing. Therefore, the office requests the immediate suspension of the decision granting provisional release and the preventive detention of the suspect until the final judgment of the appeal.



> Man kills transgender woman and her husband in Taquarussu to defend wife from assault.
> Capivara News Editorial Staff by Capivara News Editorial Staff  June 5, 2026 In Campo Grande , News , Police , Latest News
> 
> Nathalia dos Anjos Molina, a 33-year-old trans woman, and her husband Ademar Spacino Júnior, 38, were shot dead in the early hours of Friday (5), in Vila Taquarussu, in Campo Grande. The suspect in the murder was arrested and said he killed them because they assaulted his wife.
> 
> Nathalia's mother disagrees with this version of events, saying the motive was that the couple was same-sex. Family and friends of the victims also said that the suspect was considered problematic and caused trouble with everyone in the village.
> 
> Nathalia was known for helping her mother with her bar and snack business, and she didn't deserve to "die like that."
> 
> According to the police report, the perpetrator's wife stated that the victim couple spent the night consuming alcohol and drugs and that there was a history of threats, including a police report filed in March of this year. She reported that, upon leaving for work around 5:30 a.m., she was approached aggressively by the victims.
> 
> According to the woman, Nathalia delas tried to hit her with a piece of wood, while Ademar allegedly threatened to get a knife to kill her.
> 
> The suspect, identified as Deivison, told police that he went out to defend his wife and that Nathalia had tried to attack him with a piece of wood. He stated that he fired shots at the victim and then shot Ademar after seeing him with a knife in his hands. After the crime, he fled the scene and was located at his mother-in-law's house.
> 
> However, evidence gathered by forensics could be crucial in clarifying the sequence of events. According to the police report, Nathalia had three entry wounds in her back, while Ademar was hit by two shots in the chest area. A revolver and ammunition were seized at the suspect's residence.
> 
> The case was registered as simple homicide, threats, and illegal possession of a firearm. The Civil Police are investigating the circumstances of the double homicide and will determine if there was a transphobic motive, as claimed by the victims' families, or if the crime occurred after a series of threats and conflicts described by the suspects.
