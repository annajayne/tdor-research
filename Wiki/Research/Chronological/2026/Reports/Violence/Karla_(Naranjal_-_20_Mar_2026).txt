> II. CRIMINOLOGICAL ANALYSIS AND RECONSTRUCTION OF THE EVENTS
> 
> On the night of March 20, 2026, Karla and Carlos were traveling by motorcycle on the Pan-American Highway near the San Juan community in the Naranjal canton, Guayas province. According to preliminary information gathered by Observatorio Runa Sipiy through its community network and local media, the couple was intercepted by armed individuals who repeatedly opened fire on them.
> 
> The modus operandi—intercepting the victim in motion, multiple discharges of projectiles, and finishing them off on the spot—corresponds to the typical characteristics of organized contract killings, a modality that the Observatory has identified in 34% of the cases of lethal violence against trans people registered since 2022. The precision of the attack and the plurality of impacts on the victims indicate prior planning and knowledge of the victims' movement route.
> 
> Karla was transported alive by ambulance to the nearest health center; however, medical reports confirmed her death minutes later, as a result of gunshot wounds that damaged vital organs. Carlos (unofficially known to be her partner) died instantly at the scene from direct gunshot wounds to the head. Both victims had migrated from Manabí to the province of Guayas, a context of internal displacement and forced migration that exposes transgender people to greater vulnerability and a lack of support networks.
> 
> Karla's gender identity is recognized on her Ecuadorian identity document as female, which is a central legal element for classifying the act as a possible transfemicide according to the standards established by the Runa Sipiy Observatory, Consultorio Jurídico X, Estudio Jurídico Rodríguez & Elias, and international human rights frameworks. The Observatory maintains this case under the category of "possible transfemicide" until the prosecutor's investigation determines whether hatred based on Karla's gender identity was a motivating factor in the attack.
> 
> III. LEGAL ANALYSIS — RODRÍGUEZ AND ELÍAS LAW FIRM / LEGAL CONSULTING ROOM X
> 
> The Rodríguez y Elías Law Firm and the X Legal Clinic point out that the present case activates at least three dimensions of legal analysis that must be explicitly incorporated into the prosecutorial investigation:
> 
> Classification of transfemicide: Ecuador does not have a specific criminal offense for transfemicide, which forces the Prosecutor's Office to frame Karla's death within the femicide classified in article 141 of the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code (COIP) with the aggravating circumstance of discrimination based on gender identity provided for in article 45, number 11. The Runa Sipiy Observatory and its legal alliances urge the Prosecutor's Office to apply this classification explicitly in the prosecutorial investigation.
>
> Investigation of the motivation: In accordance with UN Human Rights Council Resolution 2573 and the Yogyakarta Principles, every violent death of a person of diverse gender identity must be investigated with the standard of enhanced due diligence, determining whether there was a transphobic hate component as a motivation for the crime.
>
> Organized crime context: The contract killing suggests a possible link to criminal networks operating along the Pan-American Highway corridor in Naranjal. The Prosecutor's Office must investigate whether Karla was a victim of extortion, territorial control, or any form of exploitation that disproportionately affects transgender women in situations of internal migration. This line of investigation is independent of—and does not exclude—the hypothesis of transfemicide.
> The Rodríguez y Elías Law Firm and the X Legal Clinic remain available to offer legal support to family members of victims who request it, as well as to provide technical support to the Prosecutor's Office in the correct classification of the act.
>

Continuing to count the dead without official data is institutional complicity.”
