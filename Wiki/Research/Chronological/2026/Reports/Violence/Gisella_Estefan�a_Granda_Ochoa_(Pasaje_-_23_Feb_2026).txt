> Trans Woman Murdered in Pasaje
> The victim's home is located on the corner of Juan Montalvo and Galo García streets in Pasaje. (Reference image - Canva)
> Trans Woman Found Dead in Pasaje: Calls for Investigation into Possible Hate Crime
> LGBTI+ groups are calling for the case of Gisella Granda Ochoa to be investigated as a possible hate crime.
> February 24, 2026
> 
> The absence began to worry the neighborhood. Three days without seeing Gisella enter or leave her home, three days with the door ajar. On the night of Monday, February 23, 2026, a neighbor decided to alert the police. Minutes later, officers confirmed their suspicions: Gisella Estefanía Granda Ochoa, 46, was found dead inside her bedroom.
> 
> The discovery was made around 8:00 p.m. on February 23, in a house located on the corner of Juan Montalvo and Galo García streets in the Pasaje canton. The alert came in through ECU 911 and was handled by police personnel from the Praderas precinct.
> 
> According to the official report, the body had a wound on the neck caused by a sharp object. The victim was in a state of decomposition. Officers from the Praderas mobile unit and command personnel from the Pasaje district responded to the scene, securing the area and carrying out the corresponding legal procedures for the removal of the body.
> 
> Police confirmed that she was a transgender woman who lived alone in the house and had no criminal record. The case was brought to the attention of judicial authorities for the respective investigations.
> 
> After the incident came to light, LGBTQ+ organizations issued public statements. The Silueta X Association, along with the Ecuadorian Federation of LGBT+ Organizations, the National Trans Platform, and the Quito Pink Resistance Coalition, indicated that the crime should be investigated under the protocols for potential hate crimes.
> 
> Diane Rodríguez, director of the Silueta X Association and the LGBT+ Federation, called for the formation of a specialized forensics team and for a thorough investigation to be guaranteed to identify and punish those responsible.
> 
> The groups warned that trans women face contexts of structural violence and that this case adds to other incidents recorded in recent years. They also called on the State to strengthen protection measures and ensure effective access to justice for this population.
> 
> In the neighborhood, Gisella was a well-known figure. Neighbors indicated that they did not hear any unusual noises in the days leading up to the murder, which reinforces the hypothesis that the incident occurred without anyone noticing. The victim had a salon in Pasaje, a profession for which she was recognized in the area.
> 
> In the province of El Oro, this is the first case involving the murder of a trans woman so far in 2026. In August 2025, a trans stylist was found dead in downtown Machala, in circumstances that also prompted statements from social organizations.
> 
> The investigations continue to clarify what happened and determine responsibility.
