> An unknown trans woman disappeared at the end of the year and could not be located; it was not until months later that her body was found in a state of decomposition with a severe blow to the head.



 
> Seventeen days after the case of the transfemicide of Estefanía Montiel, a 25-year-old transgender woman, was made public, the case has been archived by the Judicial System and has fallen into oblivion for the media that covered the news.
> 
> After living in a violent environment, she suffered a violent death. Even after her death, her rights continue to be violated. The case of the transgender woman from the city of Granada captured the attention of most of the country's media outlets, generating a great deal of controversy in both the Nicaraguan press and on social media.
> 
> The main form of violence faced by transgender women in Nicaragua is the denial of their gender identity. This is clearly reflected in the headlines covering Estefanía's case, where the pronoun "he" is consistently used to refer to her, demonstrating the violation of her right to be recognized as a transgender woman.
> 
> Various members of the LGBTIQ+ community took to social media to demand clarification of the motive behind her death and that justice be served so that this case does not go unpunished. Situations of aggression like these motivated groups and individuals within the LGBTQ+ community to expand the LGBTIQ+ Human Rights Agenda in Nicaragua, this time within the framework of a Central American initiative.
> 
> According to the Nicaraguan LGBTIQ+ Observatory, by the end of 2025 there will be 12 main demands from LGBTIQ+ people. The serious deterioration of the national socio-political context has generated increased uncertainty regarding the social and legal recognition of LGBTIQ+ rights, and in this context, their main demands, endorsed since 2011 in Nicaragua's Minimum Agenda for LGBTIQ+ Human Rights, have been fundamental.
> 
> In order of priority, the most pressing needs are addressed first: the right to health and comprehensive care, to equal marriage, to decent and dignified housing, to dignified work, to education without discrimination, to gender identity, to access to justice without discrimination, to political and civic participation without discrimination, to recreation, to dignified and respectful treatment in all areas of life, to physical security, and to the right to economic empowerment.
> 
> In Nicaragua, it is urgent that all these demands be addressed, as the situation for LGBTQ+ people is steadily deteriorating. According to the Nicaraguan LGBTIQ+ Observatory, five violent deaths with suspicious circumstances were recorded in 2025, not counting the numerous reports of assaults received in general.
> 
> Transgender women are the most vulnerable in the country. A prominent case is that of Frannia Alguera Espinoza, 34, originally from the municipality of San Juan del Sur, in the department of Rivas, located 106 km from the capital, Managua. Initially, it was reported that she had suffered severe head trauma and massive bleeding, the result of a brutal beating on February 26, 2025. However, the medical report determined that there were no signs of violence and that the cause of death was intoxication from illicit substances. This case was closed.
> 
> Jenifer López, a 32-year-old transgender woman from the municipality of Tuma-La Dalia, in the department of Matagalpa, located 173 km from Managua, was found dead, face up, near a gas station on the road to Waslala, on December 24, 2025. According to information gathered by the Nicaraguan LGBTIQ+ Observatory, her death was caused by a knife attack. To date, the National Police have not publicly revealed the possible motive for the crime, nor have any suspects been arrested.
> 
> Other reported victims of hate crimes include two gay men. Omar Jeremías González, 38, from Jinotega, was found hanging from the bars of a cell in the police station of Santa María de Pantasma, a municipality 176 km from the capital. His death was ruled a suicide.
> 
> José Alfredo Pérez Picado, 42, originally from the city of Matagalpa, was murdered by an unknown assailant on March 2, 2025, inside a bar in the Guanuca neighborhood of that city, located 130 km from Managua. Further information about this case, which has been classified by the National Police as a homicide, is unavailable.
> 
> The case of Estefanía Montiel marks the first homicide victim of 2026. Her body was found in a state of decomposition, so a thorough autopsy was not performed; only the initial examination at the scene was conducted. The preliminary forensic report indicates that she had been deceased for approximately five days and that the cause of death was possibly a severe blow to the head. Her remains were buried immediately.
> 
> The demands of the 2025 Agenda for equal rights for LGBTQ+ people in their daily lives help us understand the situation each individual faces. This reality varies according to their sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, as well as their age, origin, economic status, and social position.
> 
> These inequalities disproportionately affect transgender people, who, due to their visibility, face greater difficulties accessing education, obtaining decent employment, and achieving recognition of their identity. LGBTIQ+ older adults and LGBTIQ+ people with disabilities are also among the most vulnerable groups. Finally, it is important to remember that those belonging to specific ethnic groups, such as Indigenous and Afro-descendant LGBTIQ+ people, are often discriminated against not only because of their sexual orientation and gender identity, but also because of their beliefs, culture, and language.
