> INSTITUTIONAL STATEMENT — LEGAL CLINIC X / SILHOUETTE ASSOCIATION X
> 
> The Legal Clinic X , the specialized legal arm of the Silueta X Association , administrator of the Runa Sipiy Observatory since 2013 , in conjunction with the Ecuadorian Federation of LGBT+ Organizations (73 national collectives), the National Platform for Trans Revolution – CoaliTrans (22 national trans collectives) and the Quito Coalition for Pink Resistance (8 local collectives), condemns in the strongest possible terms the murder of the woman known as "La China ," a 32-year-old trans woman, who was shot dead in the street in Quevedo, Los Ríos province, on the night of April 15, 2026.
> 
> This crime is not an isolated incident. It constitutes the materialization of a systematic pattern of lethal violence against trans women in Ecuador, which the Runa Sipiy Observatory has been continuously documenting since 2013 and which the Ecuadorian State has been unable to stop, prevent, or effectively punish.
> 
> From Legal Clinic X, an agency specializing in the criminal classification of gender crimes and strategic litigation, we believe that this murder should be investigated under the criminal classification of transfemicide, in accordance with the standards of enhanced due diligence established by the Inter-American Court of Human Rights and the guidelines of the Minnesota Protocol for the investigation of potentially unlawful deaths.
> 
>  CRIMINOLOGICAL AND LEGAL-FORENSIC ANALYSIS
> 
1. > Situational context of the event
> 
> The murder of "La China" occurred in a public place, on Tenth Street in downtown Quevedo, at 11:43 p.m., a time when transgender sex workers are most active in the area. The nature of the attack—a barrage of gunfire by unknown assailants without warning—exhibits the characteristic pattern documented by the Runa Sipiy Observatory in transfemicides perpetrated with the involvement of organized crime actors or their territorial networks.
> 
2. > Applicable legal classification
> 
> From the perspective of Legal Clinic X, the case meets the criteria for classification under Article 141 of the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code (COIP) — Femicide, with the aggravating circumstance of Article 47, paragraph 11 of the COIP (crime motivated by gender identity prejudice), a classification that must be activated ex officio by the Attorney General's Office. The investigation's focus on a hate crime does not exclude the hypothesis of organized crime, but rather complements it: in Ecuador, the practice of criminal groups demanding extortionate "protection money" from transgender sex workers is documented, and their refusal to pay triggers executions.
> 
3. > Duty of enhanced due diligence
> 
> In accordance with the standards established in the case of González et al. (“Cotton Field”) v. Mexico by the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, the Ecuadorian State has an obligation to conduct investigations with a gender perspective when the victim is a woman (including transgender women). This implies: (a) not reducing the investigation to generic hypotheses of insecurity; (b) gathering contextual evidence on the situation of transgender people in the area; (c) documenting any history of prior threats; and (d) ensuring that the case file reflects the victim's gender identity as she expressed it during her lifetime.
> 
4. > Invisibility as a second death
> 
> The Runa Sipiy Observatory warns of the repeated practice by media outlets and official reports of registering murdered trans women under their legal male name, denying posthumously the gender identity they constructed in life. This practice constitutes a form of secondary institutional violence documented by the PAHO/WHO as "symbolic revictimization." Legal Clinic X demands that all state records—Prosecutor's Office, National Police, Civil Registry, INEC—record the victim's adopted name and gender identity.
>
