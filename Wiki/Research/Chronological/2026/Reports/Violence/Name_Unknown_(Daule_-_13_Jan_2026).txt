> First 'transfemicide' of 2026 reported in Ecuador; 30 murders were recorded in 2025
> LGBT+ organizations reported the first transfemicide of 2026 in Ecuador and counted 30 murders in 2025.
> 
> 
> The Silueta X Association, in coordination with the National Trans Revolution Platform (Coalitrans) and the Ecuadorian Federation of LGBT+ Organizations, denounced the first "transfemicide" of 2026, this Tuesday, January 13. In 2025, the organizations recorded 30 murders
> 
> Silueta X, the National Trans Revolution Platform (Coalitrans) and the Ecuadorian Federation of LGBT Organizations rejected the murder of a Venezuelan trans woman, known as "the Venezuelan," which occurred in the early morning of January 12 in the Daule canton, Guayas province.
> 
> According to the testimonies gathered, "the Venezuelan woman" previously resided in El Oro, where she collaborated with local foundations and was recognized as a calm person dedicated to her work, the organizations said in their statement.
> 
> At the beginning of January, she moved to Daule with other colleagues.
> 
> She worked as a sex worker independently through digital platforms.
> 
> According to LGBT+ groups, initial reports suggest the crime may have been planned under the guise of a fake service.
> 
> The organizations warned that the case highlights the double vulnerability faced by transgender migrant women in Ecuador.
> 
> This means: the lack of family support networks and exposure to environments of violence and insecurity.
> 
> They indicated that the body of "the Venezuelan woman" remains in the morgue, with difficulties in its removal because there are no direct relatives in the country to carry out the legal procedure.
> 
> In response to this, the National Trans Platform and the Ecuadorian Federation of LGBT+ Organizations demanded that the Attorney General's Office initiate an investigation.
> 
> The organizations demand that it be done under the transfeminicide protocol, considering the cruelty and the context of vulnerability.
> 
> They asked the Women and Human Rights institution to activate protection and assistance protocols and provide support for the management of the body.
> 
> They asked the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and consular authorities to facilitate the processes for a dignified burial, taking into account their status as human mobility migrants.
> 
> They demanded that the National Police clarify whether the murder was a planned attack or linked to criminal networks in Daule and its surroundings.
> 
> Germán Castillo, president of Es Mi Familia and vice president of the LGBT+ Federation, made an urgent appeal to facilitate the delivery of the body.
> 
> Diane Rodríguez, president of Silueta X and director of the LGBT+ Federation, demanded that the case be investigated as a transfeminicide and not as a common crime, underscoring the total lack of protection for the victim.
> 
> Geovanny Jaramillo, president of Bolivarianos Diversos and transmasculine spokesperson for Coalitrans, denounced a planned attack carried out under false pretenses and demanded immediate security and justice.
