> Denise Johnson imagined spending Sunday with her daughter, Sophie Lee, eating crabs at the park. Instead, she stood on a Barclay street corner Sunday evening surrounded by nearly 100 people who came to remember her daughter, a transgender woman who was fatally shot there Friday.
> 
> The 27-year-old woman was found in the 2400 block of North Calvert Street around 1:15 a.m. Friday, Baltimore Police said in a news release.
> 
> Lindsey Eldridge, a police department spokesperson, identified the woman as Sophie Felipa, but her mother said she went by Sophie Lee and was known by that name to her family and friends. Eldridge said it’s unclear if her identity had anything to do with her death and the investigation is ongoing.
> 
> Baltimore Safe Haven, an organization that offers housing services, health and wellness, community support and legal navigation for LGBTQ+ people, hosted a candlelight vigil for Lee on Sunday evening around the area where she was found.
> 
> Johnson said, despite the tragedy, it was lovely to see so many people gather for her child.
> 
> “I didn’t know she had all this help,” she said.
> 
> Johnson remembers her daughter as a lovable, kind and joyful person who enjoyed laughing, dancing and partying. Lee was known in the ballroom scene in Baltimore, a space for community building and artistic expression.
> 
> Iya Dammons, the founder and CEO of Baltimore Safe Haven, presented Johnson with a check at Sunday’s vigil to help pay for Lee’s burial. The money was raised through a GoFundMe. Johnson expressed gratitude to the organization and everyone who came out.
> 
> “I was lost. I didn’t know where I was going to get any help from,” she said.
> 
> Ja’Nia Simmons, 45, hopes Lee knew just how loved she was. Simmons met Lee roughly eight years ago through one of her “daughters” in the ballroom scene.
> 
> They kept in touch mostly via Snapchat, Simmons said. It was heartening for Simmons to watch Lee step into her femininity, she said, especially when “the world tries to tell you who you are” as a transgender woman.
> 
> “I knew she was trying to evolve as a woman,” she said. “She was loved, and her memory will live on.”
> 
> Still, it’s hard to see girls that young dying, Simmons said. For Alexis Newby, the chief operating officer of Baltimore Safe Haven, hearing about the death of a transgender woman is triggering and traumatic.
> 
> “It’s scary because anyone can be harmed, especially in this climate,” Newby said.
> 
> Newby wants more transgender people to know there’s help for them, including at Baltimore Safe Haven. She also hopes this incident illustrates for people the need for more housing support and workforce development opportunities for marginalized communities.
> 
> “Sophie deserved another birthday. Another laugh. Another chance to love, dream, and grow old,” officials from Baltimore Safe Haven wrote in a Facebook post. “Being transgender should never cost someone their life.”




> On Friday, September 11, 2026, a 27 year old Black trans woman was found dying from multiple gunshot wounds in the Charles Village neighborhood of Baltimore. Her name was Sophie Lee.
> 
> The Crime
> According to WBALTV, officers responded to a call at 1:16 a.m. and found the victim in the area of North Calvert Street near East 24th Street. She was pronounced dead at the scene.
> 
> Sources said she had been shot in a nearby alley and was found lying in the middle of the intersection, with her purse discovered two blocks away.
> 
> Police have released few details on the investigation. Anyone with information can contact Metro Crime Stoppers of Maryland anonymously at 1-866-7LOCKUP
> 
> Honoring Sophie
> Sophie Lee was born and raised in Baltimore, Maryland. She studied at the Baltimore School of the Arts. She identified as a Black trans woman and “girl who likes girls” on her socials.
> 
> Sophie have ties to The Royal Houses of Gabbanna, Voltaire, Pink Lady, and the House of Champions (House of Balenciaga). This spring, she was named Best New Overall Mother, an honor she was very proud of. Her feed is full of back and forth comments with members of her communities, celebrating life milestones as well as ballroom performances.
> 
> Sophie was an advocate, resisting erasure of trans women by the lesbian community. She posted about listening to Sade and her appreciation of music from the 70’s and 80’s. She enjoyed dancing and spoke with joy about Black Pride events.
> 
> Friends have planned a candlelight vigil on Sunday, September 13, 2026
> 
> Iya Damons CEO of Baltimore Safe Haven said “Sophie was more than what happened to her. She was a person. She was loved. She mattered. She deserved the opportunity to live, grow, laugh, love, and simply exist safely as herself. We are coming together to honor Sophie’s life, mourn her loss, and surround those who loved her with community.
> 
> The crowdfund set up to cover Sophie’s final expenses also said “Violence against transgender women cannot become something we simply accept. Sophie had a name. Sophie had a life. Sophie had people who loved her.”
> 
> The Context
> Sophie Lee is 28th person whose death we’ve memorialized in 2026. She is the 23nd trans woman, the 16th trans person of color, and the 13th black trans woman.
> 
> 28 people are on this list. 18 of them are BIPOC trans siblings. 13 were Black, two were Latina, and one was Pasifika. Eight were white, the identity of one is unknown as of now. We talk a lot about disproportionate impact – here is the evidence for those who doubt.
> 
> It is only September, but the total of these terrible deaths exceeds 2025 and comes nearer by day to the losses of 2024.
> 
> Earlier this year, we reported on the death of Asia Williams also in Baltimore. Looking back, we’ve memorialized Tai Lathan, Morgan Moore, Tasiyah ‘Siyah’ Woodland, Meghan Riley Lewis, Danny Henson. Taya Ashton, Johanna Metzger, Ashanti Carmon, Zoe Spears, Bailey Reeves, Tydie, Alphonza Watson, Keyonna Monroe Blackeney, Zella Ziona,  Kandy Hall, and Mia Henderson, That’s 18 violent deaths, most in Baltimore.
> 
> If all of this context overwhelms you, that’s appropriate. This isn’t intended to be a tidy little tribute that makes readers feel sad, but better. Reach into that overwhelm and do something with it.



> Baltimore police are investigating the death of a transgender woman found in Charles Village, as community members mourn her loss and call for answers.
> 
> Homicide detectives were seen knocking on doors Friday morning, asking residents for home security footage and information.
> 
> Police said no arrests have been made, no suspects are in custody, and a motive has not been determined. They also stated they are not releasing the victim's name, saying, "We have not positively identified the victim."
> 
> Leaders at Baltimore Safe Haven, a local organization, expressed shock and grief over the loss. They said the victim was a friend and former client, and her death has deeply affected their staff.
> 
> "This is not only a former client of ours, but is also a friend of many of the people here on our staff, and it has affected us greatly, greatly, greatly," Renee Lau from Safe Haven said. "We had to have a meeting this morning to make sure staff was OK to even start to open the doors this morning."
> 
> The organization is planning a candlelight vigil near the scene where the woman was found.
> 
> They also voiced frustration over the limited information being released by police and expressed concerns about safety in the community.
> 
> "This is a scary thing for people of trans experience because we don’t know exactly what happened," another person said. "She could have gotten murdered because she was trans. So this could strike up a lot of different feelings inside of individuals. And we have the behavior and mental health component here that we’re able to address it."
> 
> Police reported that officers responded to a call at 1:16 a.m. and found the victim in the area of North Calvert Street near East 24th Street. She was pronounced dead at the scene.
> 
> Sources said she had been shot in a nearby alley and was found lying in the middle of the intersection, with her purse discovered two blocks away.
> 
> Safe Haven urged anyone emotionally affected by the incident to reach out for support. They also called on police to collaborate with them.
