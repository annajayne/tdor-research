> "She was always considered a woman, but gender doesn't matter; it was male violence," said the family of Franyel, who was murdered in Marbella.
> The victim was preparing a trip to reunite with his family in Venezuela, who had lost contact with him two days before the crime.
> "That man wouldn't let her be herself or go out," her relatives assert.
> August 14, 2026 - 10:18
> 
> Frayelys Molina is one of the sisters of the 35-year-old trans woman murdered this weekend , allegedly by her 59-year-old partner, in the home they shared in San Pedro Alcántara, Marbella . She spoke to Málaga Hoy from Venezuela , across the ocean, where her family had hoped to see her again after almost six years.
> 
> Franyel was the name still listed on her documents; Kimberlyn was the female term she used to identify. According to her sister, the victim "always considered herself a woman" and argues that her murder should be recognized as gender-based violence.
> 
> The last conversation between the two sisters dates back to last Friday, two days before the stabbing. The victim was preparing to return to her country when she stopped communicating. "She went to get her things from this man's house and never wrote to me again. She wanted to come and see us," she told this newspaper.
> 
> Franyel's family was slow to learn of the tragedy. It was a friend living in Spain who informed them after learning what had happened through the media. On Wednesday, the police confirmed the fatal outcome. Following his arrest, his partner remains in pretrial detention, under investigation for suspected homicide.
> 
> "Every time I called her, he was by my side; she was never alone."
> For his sister, the crime stems from a desire for control and a toxic relationship that had recently begun to worry his family and friends in Venezuela. “Towards the end, I noticed he was acting very suspiciously,” the victim’s sister reflects. “Every time I called him, he was by my side; he was never alone,” she adds.
> 
> Sources from the High Court of Justice of Andalusia (TSJA) reported this week that the two had filed complaints against each other shortly before. Frayelys argues that these confrontations stemmed from the fact that "the man wouldn't let her work anymore." Both were arrested on August 1st, but neither subsequently ratified the complaint, and the court dismissed the preliminary proceedings.
> 
> The woman met the man who would soon become her sister's killer via video call a couple of months ago . She recalls that she quickly realized the relationship wasn't going well: "She told me she was tired of that man because he wouldn't let her be herself or go out. She wouldn't even work because he would start a fight."
> 
> Although Franyel never warned her to be afraid, she does believe he conveyed it in another way. “He didn’t tell me exactly that in words, but he did use gestures,” she emphasizes.
> 
> "The pillar of the house"
> Franyel's sister recalls that she was also the family's breadwinner in Venezuela. "She was the pillar of the house, the one who helped us," she told this newspaper. Her mother's death had been another blow that led her to dedicate herself even more to her family.
> 
> The family now faces another obstacle: they want her sister's murder recognized as gender-based violence. For Frayelys, the administrative dispute doesn't change who her sister was. She hadn't completed the legal gender change process in Spain, but she was in the process of doing so. "She considered herself a woman; she was getting the paperwork done," she explains. The fact that she hadn't legally changed her gender has had consequences for how the case is being handled in court, as it has been referred to a general court rather than a specialized court for violence against women. This has provoked a reaction from trans organizations. The Trans Association of Andalusia-Sylvia Rivera (ATA) demands that this lack of documentation not leave Franyel without protection against gender-based violence.
> 
> The organization seeks to have the murder recognized as gender-based violence, argues that the woman cannot continue to be a victim after being murdered , and denounces that the administrative delay for foreign trans people to adapt their documentation in Spain should not now become an argument to deny their identity.
> 
> In a joint petition with the Women's Care Team (EAM), the group argues that the lack of registration modification cannot make a trans migrant woman a person excluded from protection against gender violence.
> 
> ATA points out that Franyel was affected by an administrative delay related to the proper documentation of foreign transgender people. Article 50 of Law 4/2023 recognizes this right, but the procedure to implement it in documentation issued in Spain was not activated until July 2026.
> 
> The association believes that this discrepancy in documentation cannot be used to erase Franyel's identity after her death. In its petition to the institutions, it demands that her identity as a woman be taken into consideration, that the existence of gender-based violence within the relationship be assessed, and that it be guaranteed that no transgender migrant woman is excluded from protection due to administrative obstacles beyond her control: “Franyel cannot be administratively erased after she has been murdered.”
> 
> Now the family wants Franyel to come home. But even after death, the money for repatriating the body has become an obstacle. Frayelys explains that transporting the body is expensive and that the family has limited resources. Their intention is to authorize a trusted person in Spain to handle the arrangements, retrieve the body, and transport it back to their homeland.
> 
> "He wasn't a good man to her."
> Speaking anonymously in Brazil, a childhood friend also shared her story. Franyel was the godmother of her 13-year-old son . Their last words were exchanged on August 9, three days before she was stabbed to death in San Pedro de Alcántara.
> 
> She's still trying to process the news. "She was more than a friend, very dear to everyone," she says. She remembers her as a woman "always ready to help her family in Venezuela." Although she admits she doesn't know the details of the crime, she notes that Franyel had told her on several occasions "that that man wasn't very good to her."


> Franyel was 35 years old and had been in a relationship for barely three months with the man who ended up stabbing her to death.
> 
> It was on Sunday, August 9, in Marbella. She was Venezuelan, she was a trans woman, and she had spent years waiting for Spain to finally recognize her as what she was: a woman. The 2023 Trans Law allowed it in theory, but the procedure for foreigners was not activated until July of this year, and Franyel ran out of time.
> 
> That is why, when she was killed, the High Court of Justice of Andalusia decided that the case would not be investigated by a gender violence court, but by a general investigating court. The Police recorded it as domestic violence. The 58-year-old man, her partner, is in prison without bail. Mar Cambrollé, president of the Trans Association of Andalusia, put it bluntly: she was murdered as a woman, and the State that denied her the paperwork now also denies her justice.



> Franyel, a 35-year-old Venezuelan trans woman, was stabbed to death by her partner last Sunday, August 9, in San Pedro Alcántara, Marbella (Málaga, Spain). The alleged assailant, a 59-year-old man, confessed to the crime and was arrested by the National Police and remanded in custody.
> 
> This Wednesday, August 12, the Spanish Trans Association of Andalusia-Sylvia Rivera (ATA) demanded that the murder of Franyel be recognized and treated institutionally as a crime of gender violence.
> 
> The NGO argued that Franyel was "the victim of a chain of violence that cannot continue after her death" and called on both the Spanish Ministries of Equality and Justice and the competent judicial authorities to address her murder as gender-based violence.
> 
> ATA President Mar Cambrollé argued that this woman was a victim of both the violence perpetrated by her partner and the "negligence of a government that took more than three years from the approval of the Trans Law to enable the procedure that would allow trans migrants to effectively exercise their right to adjust the sex on their documentation."
> 
> Therefore, she argued that "she cannot now be a victim again of institutions that, precisely because they prevented her from making that change, refuse to recognize her as a woman and to treat her murder as gender-based violence."
> 
> She also demanded "criteria that guarantee that no trans migrant woman is left out of protection against gender violence for a purely registration issue."
> 
> For ATA, it is "especially serious" that this "documentary discrepancy, a consequence of years of administrative inaction", can be used to deny Franyel, once murdered, her status as a woman and the recognition of the sexist violence she suffered.
> 
> "Franyel was a woman, she lived as a woman, and she was murdered as a woman," Cambrollé stated, emphasizing that "real equality will never be built on the exclusion or forgetting of any woman."



> A 59-year-old man has been arrested in San Pedro Alcántara (Málaga) for the stabbing death of his partner, a 35-year-old transgender woman of Venezuelan origin . According to Málaga Hoy , the suspect confessed to the crime to a neighbor who alerted the local police and later admitted it to officers of the National Police . The attack took place on Monday afternoon at a house on Calle Castilla, in the Los Catalanes area.
> 
> Emergency services received the alert around 4:15 p.m. Despite the rapid mobilization of police and medical teams, nothing could save the victim's life; she had serious stab wounds to the chest . The couple had been living together in the same property for some time. According to police testimony gathered from neighbors, arguments between the two were common , and law enforcement had been called out on several occasions for this reason.
> 
> The case is being investigated as a domestic violence homicide, as the victim had not officially begun the process of changing the gender marker on their documents. There is no record that the couple was married. The suspect is in custody awaiting a court appearance, and the investigation remains open to clarify what actually happened.




> He murdered his partner, a 35-year-old trans woman, with several stab wounds in Marbella (Malaga)
> The victim was found dead in a house on calle Castilla in San Pedro de Alcántara with a serious chest wound; investigators are working on the hypothesis of a possible gender-based crime
> SER Málaga11/08/2026 - 06:00 CEST
> 
> Another violent death has occurred in the province of Málaga. A 35-year-old woman was found dead Monday afternoon in a home in San Pedro Alcántara, Marbella, with injuries consistent with a knife attack. According to police sources who spoke to SER radio, officers have arrested a 58-year-old man in connection with the incident. The investigation remains open to determine exactly what happened and clarify the circumstances of his death.
> 
> The call came in around 4:15 pm Emergency services in Andalusia (112) mobilized medical personnel after receiving a report that a woman had been assaulted inside a home on Calle Castilla, in the heart of San Pedro Alcántara. National Police officers and paramedics were dispatched to the building, where they found the seriously injured victim.
> 
> The woman had at least one extremely serious chest injury. Paramedics attempted to assist her at the scene, but could only confirm her death. The investigation was immediately taken over by the National Police, who carried out the initial inquiries and subsequently arrested a man allegedly connected to the death.
> 
> Police sources indicate that the victim and the detainee had a relationship, a detail currently being analyzed by investigators. Apparently, arguments between the two were frequent, and the alleged perpetrator confessed to the crime. At this time, authorities have not officially confirmed that it was a case of gender-based violence, and the investigation will determine the nature and motive of the murder. The detainee remains in custody while investigators gather evidence and testimonies to reconstruct the events.
> 
> The crime also occurred in a province experiencing one of the most tragic periods in recent years regarding violence against women. Málaga has now seen six women murdered by their partners or ex-partners in 2026, after the Government Delegation against Gender Violence confirmed last Friday that the case of Melinda, a 45-year-old Hungarian woman murdered in Benahavís on July 21, was a gender-based crime.
> 
> With that confirmation, Malaga equaled in just seven months the total number of victims recorded during all of 2015, when the province reached its historical peak since official records began in 2003. Nationally, the number of confirmed gender-based violence murders so far this year amounts to 36.
> 
> The case in San Pedro Alcántara, however, is not yet included in those official statistics. The National Police are continuing their investigation to determine if there is a direct link between the death and the romantic relationship between the victim and the detainee.
> 
> The latest death comes after a particularly grim July in Málaga, with several homicides of women under investigation in different parts of the province. Among them is the murder of Melinda in Benahavís, initially ruled out as a gender-based violence crime by the first investigations, but later officially confirmed as such.
> 
> Forensic tests, the autopsy, and all the police investigations will now determine precisely how the woman died and what responsibility the arrested man had. The investigation remains confidential, and further actions are possible in the coming hours.
