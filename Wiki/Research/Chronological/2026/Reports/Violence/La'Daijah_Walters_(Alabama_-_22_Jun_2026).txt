> 32 Year Old Black Trans Woman La’Daijah Walters Fatally Shot In Alabama
> June 23, 2026 by Sue
> La’Daijah Walters was shot and killed in Mobile, Alabama
> 
> The Crime
> WKRG reports:
> 
> Mobile police have confirmed a [person] was shot and killed the morning at [of June 22] Plantation Estates apartments. According to an MPD news release, the shooting happened around 6:35 a.m. inside an apartment unit at 5725 Old Pascagoula Road.
> 
> No information on a motive or suspect has been released.
> 
> Anyone with any information that could assist in the investigation of this case is asked to contact the Mobile Police Department at 251-208-7211. Alternatively, you can submit an anonymous tip by texting 844-251-0644 or by visiting mobilepd.org/crimetip.
> 
> Early media reports seem to have appropriately identified her as a woman, then updated to misgender her. It is astounding that a credible journalist or media outlet cannot put those facts together and expand their reporting outside of the binary. This once again forces those grieving her to have to advocate for accurate and fair reporting. It is an unfair burden.
> 
> Honoring La’Daijah
> La’Daijah was 32 years old, She was born and raised in Mobile, Alabama where she attended Murphy High School.
> 
> Her socials reference the everyday things we take for granted such as going to see the Mortal Kombat movie, wish her brother a happy birthday, and lots of irreverent, fun memes and reels. She also shared experiences of anti-trans words and deeds that weave through her content, a thread that she intentionally wraps in her defiance, her joy, and her determination to live her authentic self.
> 
> She spent time in Houston, Pensacola, and Atlanta. She was enthusiastically alive, a vibrant presence to her friends and community.
> It seems she had some ties to the ballroom community. She was also part of the [Black Butterfly Collective](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=61556927026122).
> 
> Her name has been spelled La’Daijah (on her social accounts) but also Ladaijah, LaDayjah, and Ladasia by her loved ones.
> 
> Her friend Myah Turner posted a gut wrenching tribute on Facebook.
>

> 
> Far too many Black transgender women have been taken from us. Far too many families, friends, and communities have had to carry this grief. We are tired of watching our people become statistics. We are tired of seeing our sisters become headlines.Today, we mourn. We grieve. We hold one another a little tighter.

> Rest in power, La’Daijah. We must work hard to ensure justice and accountability from the person who took your life. You deserve that final dignity. You made an impact in 32 years. It is our loss that we will never know what you would have done with the next 32. Thank you for the joy and realness you brought to this world.
> 
> May your memory be a revolution.




> I know many people will begin sharing Ladaijah’s photo, asking what happened, and expressing their thoughts and feelings. As those conversations unfold, I ask that we all hold grace.
> Ladaijah was not just a headline. She was not just a social media post. She was a person who was deeply known in Mobile, loved by family, both chosen and biological, and connected to a community that is now grieving.
> This loss hits especially close to home for The Black Butterfly Collective. Ladaijah was one of our trans sisters. One of our own. One of the people who made up the fabric of our community.
> For some, this may be another news story. For us, this is family.
> As a leader in the transgender community, you don’t always get the opportunity to simply be a friend. Often, you become a protector, a provider, a resource, a mentor, or the person people call when things go wrong. Because you’ve survived so much, you spend a lot of time trying to warn younger girls about the things that can harm them. Sometimes that is received as love. Sometimes it is received as judgment. Sometimes it creates distance.
> That was true for La’daijah and me.
> There were things I didn’t always agree with, and there were times when I spoke honestly because I wanted her safe. At some point, that created distance between us. But it never stopped me from checking on her. It never stopped me from making sure she was okay. It never stopped me from praying for her protection. It never stopped me from caring.
