> The death of a transvestite made invisible by the official narrative and journalistic accounts
> Cafú was 34 years old and a trans woman. On September 19, she was intentionally burned alive in Orán and died a week later. The local LGBT+ community and the Trans Movement of Argentina are demanding justice, respect for gender identity, and media coverage free of discriminatory bias.
> 
> October 4, 2026 - 05:10
> 
> Cafú was 34 years old, a trans woman, and lived in San Ramón de la Nueva Orán. She died on September 26 after spending several days hospitalized with severe burns suffered in an incident that is under investigation. A 29-year-old woman was arrested and linked to the case. Trans and gender-diverse organizations are demanding justice and respect for her self-perceived gender identity.
> 
> “We all knew her as La Cafú ,” said Ivana González , coordinator and one of the representatives of the LGBT community of Orán.
> 
> González explained that La Cafú presented herself socially as a woman and that this was how she was recognized by those around her. “She worked as a woman, spoke like a woman, and behaved like a woman. When they found her, she was wearing women's clothing and is considered a transvestite. I don't think she underwent a transition process, but she called herself La Cafú,” she recounted.
> 
> Ivana González clarified that the community does not seek to impose labels on each person's identity. In the case of La Cafú, she maintained that she was recognized as a trans woman and that her chosen name was part of how she presented herself and was known in Orán.
> 
> For her, the fact that La Cafú has not made a registered change of her name does not allow us to ignore her gender identity.
> 
> “Many girls don’t change their documents for different reasons. Sometimes they don’t make the change when they are minors. Many are misinformed about the paperwork and don’t complete that part because of the procedures, mainly because of everything the process entails, especially in these times when they are trying to take away our rights, all those things, even more so ,” she explained.
> 
> A life that is not reduced to consumption
> Those who knew La Cafú in Orán also remember other aspects of her life. González said she was looking for work.
> 
> “Beyond the fact that he had a substance abuse problem (...) he was a very kind, attentive person, always looking for work,” he recalled.
> 
> González recounted that he had been seeing her more frequently on the street for approximately two years and that Cafú was going through a problematic substance use situation.
> 
> For González, substance abuse shouldn't be the sole explanation for La Cafú's life. Her situation should also be viewed in relation to the conditions that led to her vulnerability, she reflected.
> 
> The concept of social transvesticide
> Pía Ceballos, from the Trans Movement Argentina , explained that for transvestite and trans organizations, the death of La Cafú can be analyzed from the concept of social transvesticide.
> 
> “When we talk about social transvesticide, we are talking about a concept that describes the accumulation of structural violence and exclusion that a transvestite or trans person suffers throughout their life ,” she explained.
> 
> Ceballos placed within this chain the difficulties in accessing healthcare, housing, education, and formal employment , compounded by early expulsion from homes and other social spaces. According to him, these conditions can lead to situations of poverty, marginalization, and greater vulnerability.
> 
> “This means that there is a death that could have been avoided ,” he said.
> 
> “In the case of this colleague from Orán who suffered from addiction problems, was in a situation of poverty, abandonment, etc. Clearly, one can see elements that constitute a social transvesticide ,” she stated.
> 
> Ceballos also pointed out that this category is used by organizations and activists to describe a chain of violence that is not always considered in the same way by the judicial system.
> 
> The body and identity in the face of justice
> The discussion about identity also extends to judicial investigations. Ceballos questioned why, when the body of a transvestite or trans person is found, the initial analysis might be limited to official records or genitalia.
> 
> “When they find the body of a transvestite trans person, it is not read as a transvestite trans body, it is immediately read as a male body, the genitalia are read ,” he argued.
> 
> For Ceballos, an investigation must also consider gender identity, clothing, body modifications and, especially, the way in which the person was recognized in their environment.
> 
> In the case of La Cafú, González maintained that this recognition existed long before her death became news. For those who knew her, she was La Cafú, and she was a trans woman.
> 
> The circumstances of her death remain under investigation. According to initial reports, a 29-year-old woman was arrested and linked to the case. The investigation seeks to establish how the incident occurred that resulted in her severe burns.
> 
> González stated that, according to what was circulating among members of the community, there had been a prior argument between La Cafú and a woman with whom La Cafú had already had an altercation. According to this version of events, both women were drinking alcohol when the accused attacked her.
> 
> “Essentially, what happened is supposedly what everyone is talking about,” he explained.
> 
> He also noted that the police did not provide all the information and that they were analyzing cameras that could provide data to reconstruct what happened.
> 
> "As far as I know, there are no witnesses," she replied when asked about the existence of people who had directly witnessed the event.
> 
> The investigation is being led by prosecutor María Soledad Filtrín Cuezzo .
> 
> González recalled other cases of trans women murdered in Orán that, to her knowledge, never reached a judicial resolution. Among those cases, she specifically mentioned that of a friend who was stabbed to death.
> 
> In that context, he argued that the case of La Cafú must continue to be investigated and that the circumstances of her death cannot be reduced to mere versions.
> 
> The community also has concerns about how these cases are presented. While the judicial investigation must establish what happened, the victim's identity is also part of the narrative.
> 
> The death of La Cafú reignited the debate surrounding the treatment of transgender and transvestite people in the news. The Public Prosecutor's Office confirmed her death, using the term "person." However, some media outlets identified her as male, while others identified her as female. Her transgender identity was not mentioned.
> 
> González said he learned of what happened through the media when La Cafú was still alive and dying.
> 
> “I think I found out four days later.” “I didn’t know, because they kept saying ‘male,’ ‘male,’ all the news reports said ‘male,’” she recounted.
> 
> Later, other women from the community approached her to ask why the media referred to La Cafú using masculine pronouns.
> 
> For Ceballos, respect for gender identity should be part of journalistic coverage from the very beginning.
> 
> “I think it is extremely important that there is an approach that respects gender identity, the gender identity law, but above all, that transvestite and trans people are not exposed in masculine terms,” she said.
> 
> González also suggested that the media should seek information at the scene of the events, consult neighbors and people nearby, and not just reproduce what has been published by other media outlets.
> 
> In their view, telling the story of who the victim was is also part of responsible reporting. It's not just about reporting how they died, but about preventing a person from being reduced to a file, a police case, or a substance abuse problem.
> 
> “We would have to add one more case of transvestites to the already high numbers in the province and emphasize that misinformation often causes harm ,” González stated. “Above all, we are people,” she expressed.
> 
> She also considered that, in the face of the media coverage that identified her in the male, one way to repair the damage would be to acknowledge the mistake, "to apologize."




> Details revealed about the man from Salta who died after being burned by a woman
> He remained hospitalized in critical condition since Saturday, September 19. A 29-year-old woman was arrested and charged with attempted murder; following his death, the Prosecutor's Office announced it would expand the charges.
>
>
> Terrible news was confirmed this Saturday in San Ramón de la Nueva Orán . The 34-year-old man who had been hospitalized in intensive care after being found with severe burns on different parts of his body has died .
> 
> The victim, identified by her initials MAR , had been hospitalized since Saturday, September 19 , when the incident occurred in the vicinity of Moro Díaz and Zárate streets , in the area of ​​the Abraham Balut neighborhood field.
> 
> For a week, his condition remained critical due to the extent of his injuries. Finally, on Saturday, September 26, his death was confirmed .
> 
> A 29-year-old woman remains in custody in connection with the incident , suspected of having doused him with alcohol and subsequently set him on fire after an argument.
> 
> The incident occurred on the morning of Saturday the 19th. Around 11:30 a.m. , authorities were alerted to the presence of a man with visible burns on his upper body.
> 
> When emergency services and police arrived, the victim received initial assistance. According to information obtained during the investigation, he was still conscious at that time .
> 
> He was later transferred to the hospital in Oran.
> 
> Doctors found burns on his face, neck, shoulders, chest, and hands . Due to the severity and extent of his injuries, he was immediately admitted to intensive care and his condition was considered critical.
> 
> He remained there for the following days, until this Saturday the feared outcome occurred.
> 
> Initial investigations established that the man had been meeting with a woman in the vicinity of the location.
> 
> According to the hypothesis being investigated by the Prosecutor's Office, an argument took place between the two .
> 
> In the midst of that confrontation, the suspect allegedly doused him with alcohol and then set him on fire , causing the injuries that ultimately led to his death.
> 
> The exact mechanics of the episode remain under investigation, and authorities are still trying to determine what sparked the argument.
> 
> Authorities are also trying to determine if the people involved were under the influence of alcohol or other substances at the time of the incident.
> 
> After the police arrived, investigators began searching for people who had witnessed what happened.
> 
> According to the data initially collected, several witnesses pointed to a woman as the alleged perpetrator of the attack .
> 
> The suspect was located nearby and arrested.
> 
> The investigation was placed in the hands of the criminal prosecutor of the Unit for Serious Attacks against Persons in Orán, María Soledad Filtrín Cuezzo .
> 
> The 29-year-old woman was provisionally charged as the perpetrator of the crime of attempted simple homicide .
> 
> Death changes the legal landscape
> The death of the victim will now produce a major change in the case.
> 
> Prosecutor Filtrín Cuezzo announced that the charges against the detained woman will be expanded next Monday , because the victim died as a result of the condition being investigated.
> 
> Until now, the charge had been for attempted homicide because the man was still alive.
> 
> The new legal classification must be formalized by the Prosecutor's Office taking into account the death.
