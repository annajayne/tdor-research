> LGBTIQ+ rights activist Gabriela Denisse Banach (47) was found dead Tuesday night in her apartment at Cerrito 600, in the La Sexta neighborhood in the macrocenter of Rosario.
> 
> Authorities arrived at the scene after a neighbor reported seeing the body on their balcony floor. According to initial reports, the victim died from a blow to the head, and investigators are looking into a possible transphobic hate crime .
> 
> Local media confirmed that some neighbors reported hearing noises and screams coming from the apartment where Gabriela lived on Sunday, and that nothing was heard from the activist on Monday and Tuesday.
> 
> It wasn't until Tuesday night, around 10:30 p.m., that one of his neighbors saw the victim's body lying on the balcony from his apartment and notified the police.
> 
> Police sources confirmed that upon entering the activist's apartment, investigators found the body of the trans woman, who had been beaten to death. In addition, there was smashed furniture and overturned chairs, suggesting a struggle had taken place between Banach and her attacker.
> 
> At the crime scene, the police doctor confirmed that the body had abrasions on its lower limbs and both knees. It also suffered facial trauma.
> 
> Gabriela Banach, the trans activist murdered in Rosario.Gabriela Banach, the trans activist murdered in Rosario.
> This Wednesday, after the autopsy, the forensic experts of the Legal Medical Institute determined that the cause of death was a skull fracture .
> 
> As part of the investigation, officers also discovered that a television was missing from the apartment and that the bedroom had been ransacked. According to La Voz , investigators are also working on a potentially key piece of information: the apartment door was reportedly locked.
> 
> Currently, investigators are analyzing surveillance footage from the area through the Lince system, as it could help reconstruct who entered and left the apartment in the hours leading up to Banach's death. The prosecutor in charge of the case, Rodrigo Urriticoechea, is working with the hypothesis that it was a transphobic hate crime.
> 
> Meanwhile, the Public Prosecutor's Office (MPA) confirmed to Rosario3 this Wednesday that the death of the trans activist was a crime and not a "death of dubious cause" .
> 
> After the news broke, the Association of Transvestites, Transsexuals and Transgender People of Argentina (ATTTA) condemned the activist's death: "We are not talking about a generic security incident. We are talking about a hate crime ," they stated in a press release.
> 
> They also called for a demonstration this Thursday at 11:30 a.m. in San Martín Square in Rosario, in front of the Santa Fe Government Headquarters, to demand justice for the crime. "They keep killing us. They keep taking our lives for who we are, while the violence tries to reduce our existence to statistics and silence. We will not accept living in fear as normal," they declared.
