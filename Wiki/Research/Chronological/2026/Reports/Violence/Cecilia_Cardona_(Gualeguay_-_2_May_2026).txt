> Gualeguay: A transport driver from Crespo is being investigated for the death of a trans woman
> 
> The death of Cecilia, a 47-year-old trans woman, which occurred in the early hours of May 1st at the intersection of routes 11 and 12, in Gualeguay, is being investigated by the Justice system under different hypotheses, including that of a homicide aggravated by her gender identity.
> 
> The initial report to the police indicated a possible traffic accident: a truck driver had allegedly run over the victim without noticing and continued driving. Cecilia's body lay on the asphalt. "The incident came to light around 1 am, when police officers responded to the scene after discovering a deceased person," reported the newspaper El Debate Pregón.
> 
> However, as the initial investigations progressed, doubts began to emerge about that initial version.
> 
> The case was assigned to prosecutor Mariángeles Schell, who went to the scene and ordered an autopsy. She also ordered a series of measures that allowed progress in reconstructing the events.
> 
> The truck driver, a man in his fifties who works for a company in Crespo, did not stop after the incident. He was located hours later near General Galarza and was taken into custody at the Gualeguay Police Headquarters.
> 
> As the hours passed, the hypothesis of a simple accident began to lose traction. One line of investigation suggests that the victim had a prior encounter with the truck driver and that, after an argument related to payment for sexual services, the man tried to leave.
> 
> According to the preliminary reconstruction, Cecilia attempted to prevent the vehicle from leaving by grabbing onto it, at which point she was dragged and subsequently run over. If this sequence of events is confirmed, the accidental death hypothesis would be definitively ruled out.
> 
> The case sparked a strong reaction from social organizations. Diversidad Gualeguay demanded that the case be investigated with a gender and diversity perspective, and that the incident not be reduced to a mere traffic accident.
> 
> "Cecilia died at work. She died exposed to the violence and precariousness faced by so many trans women," they stated in a press release. They also demanded that, should a motive linked to hatred based on gender identity be proven, the act be classified as a transvesticide or transfemicide.
> 
> The organization also stressed the need for public policies that guarantee dignified living conditions for the trans population, noting that many people continue to be exposed to situations of extreme vulnerability.
> 
> At this time, the Prosecutor's Office has not yet determined a definitive legal classification, although it has been confirmed that the investigation remains open under multiple hypotheses. Expert reports, testimonies, and forensic results will be key to determining responsibility and the legal framework for the case.
> 
> Meanwhile, relatives of the victim—two brothers residing in Buenos Aires—transported his remains for burial.




> An “accident” being investigated as a transvesticide
> adminMay 2, 2026
>  
> The incident occurred at the intersection of routes 11 and 12, in Gualeguay, and was reported in the early hours of Friday the 1st.
> 
> The first alert to the police was that it was an “accident”: a truck driver ran over and killed a transgender woman who worked as a prostitute, initially unaware of the tragic event. Her body lay on the road, and the truck driver continued on his way, seemingly oblivious to what had happened.
> 
> “The incident came to light around 1 a.m., when police responded to the discovery of a deceased person on the road. According to initial reports, the accident involved a large truck, whose driver apparently did not notice the impact at the time,” reported the website El Debate/Pregón.
> 
> However, due to the characteristics of the event and the evidence collected at the scene, the first doubts began to arise regarding the classification of "accident".
> 
> Upon learning of the incident, prosecutor Mariángeles Schell, from the Gualeguay Prosecutor's Office, arrived and, as one of the first measures, ordered an autopsy of the body.
> 
> One detail stood out: the truck driver didn't stop, he continued driving, and was located in Galarza. He is a man in his fifties; the victim, meanwhile, was between 40 and 50 years old.
> 
> He was immediately apprehended and as the hours passed, he was placed in pretrial detention at the Gualeguay Police Headquarters.
> 
> The measure is due to the initial suspicions surrounding the "accident" that occurred in the early hours of Friday in Gualeguay.
> 
> The initial hypothesis is that it was a homicide with aggravating circumstances due to the victim's gender identity, which could lead to the classification of transvesticide. However, the Prosecutor's Office has not yet closed the case with any definitive classification.
> 
> One of the possible lines of investigation aims to determine if there was a prearranged sexual relationship between the truck driver and the trans woman, which later led to an argument in which the man allegedly refused to pay for the service provided.
> 
> That's when he decided to flee in the truck. It was at that moment that the trans woman tried to stop him. It is presumed that she grabbed onto the driver's side door to prevent his escape, and she was dragged and run over by the vehicle. This is one of the hypotheses being investigated as a possible cause of death.
> 
> That would rule out the classification of "accident".
> 
> After the autopsy was performed, and two brothers of the trans woman who live in Buenos Aires were contacted, they arrived in Gualeguay and took the remains to be buried in the place where she was originally from.
> 
> Meanwhile, the judicial investigation remains open.
