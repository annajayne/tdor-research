> Bianca dos Santos, known by her social name Bianca, died inside her home in the Santa Libânia neighborhood of Bragança Paulista. The transgender woman, who worked as a housekeeper and lived alone with seven dogs, left friends, neighbors, and loved ones deeply touched by her way of life, marked by good humor, a willingness to help, and the affection she devoted to animals.
> 
> A neighbor and the employer found Bianca lifeless on Wednesday (3), at the residence located on Rua Santa Cruz. As the cleaning lady did not show up for work, the disappearance caught the attention of people close to her. The neighbor, who had a copy of the key, entered the property together with the employer and found Bianca on the bed.
> 
> Due to the advanced state of decomposition, initial indications suggest that the death occurred several days prior. Examinations by the Forensic Medical Institute are now expected to clarify the cause of death.
> 
> Bianca had lived in the Santa Libânia neighborhood for about 30 years and had made many friends during that time. Journalist and radio broadcaster Luci Miranda, a friend of the cleaning lady, stated that Bianca faced prejudice throughout her life, but built relationships based on affection and respect.
> 
> “Bianca was a symbol of resilience. As a trans woman, she faced prejudice her whole life, but earned respect and many friends. She will be remembered for her wide smile, her good humor, and her willingness to help. She joked about her own life challenges and had a great love for animals and plants. Despite her simple home, she maintained a beautiful and very well-kept garden,” she reported.
> 
> Furthermore, people close to her said that Bianca had been facing health problems in recent weeks, mainly due to diabetes. An employee of a shop on Rua Santa Cruz said that she last spoke to her on Friday (29). On that occasion, the cleaning lady commented that she was not feeling well.
> 
> The Animal Welfare Division (DIBEM) has taken over the care of the seven dogs that lived with Bianca. However, the technical team decided to keep them temporarily in their own home. Professionals bring water and food daily and monitor the animals' conditions.
> 
> According to the agency, the dogs are healthy and show no signs of mistreatment. However, they still show fear and avoid approaching other people.
> 
> Luci Miranda monitored the teams' work and explained that the animals seem to miss their owner.
> 
> “They are resistant to approaching anyone other than Bianca. She was the one who cared for and fed them all. The dogs are strong and well cared for, but they lost the person they trusted. Now it will be necessary to gradually earn that trust,” she stated.
> 
> Bianca lived in a house with a large yard, trees, vegetation, and old, partially demolished buildings. Because of this, dogs find many hiding places, making a quick approach difficult.
> 
> Given this scenario, the teams from Samuvet and the Animal Welfare Division preferred not to force the removal of the animals. This way, they avoid stress and potential injuries.
> 
> As Luci explained, the professionals visit the location frequently and offer food and treats so that the dogs associate the presence of the teams with a positive experience. The expectation is to gradually gain the animals' trust.
> 
> Meanwhile, the largest dog is already showing more friendly behavior and even accompanied the visitors to the exit of the house. The others, however, remain hidden among the vegetation and bark when they notice the presence of strangers.
> 
> The animals will be neutered and then put up for adoption.
> After gaining the dogs' trust, the teams will safely remove them from the residence. Following this, the animals will undergo neutering/spaying and enter the responsible adoption process.
> 
> According to people following the case, the dogs are likely to find new homes separately. After all, few families would be able to take in all seven animals at the same time.
