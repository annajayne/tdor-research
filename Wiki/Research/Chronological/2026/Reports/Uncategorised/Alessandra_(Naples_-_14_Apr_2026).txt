> Alessandra, a 47-year-old trans woman who recently died, is being fondly remembered by the Naples Transsexual Association. The ATN itself announced her passing in a social media post, which expressed its condolences and also expressed its regret: "Once again, her identity has been disrespected."
> 
> According to the association, the funeral notices reported a male first name, even though Alessandra (nicknamed “Coniglietta”) had always lived and presented herself as a woman.
> 
> The memory of the Atn
> "A companion at just 47 years old, a longtime friend of ATN, she was always there with her support, her kindness, and her strength," reads the message released by the association. A memory that emphasizes the human bond and shared commitment, but also the sense of emptiness left by her passing.
> 
> The ATN emphasizes that Alessandra "has always lived and displayed her subjectivity as feminine," highlighting the contrast with what is written on the funeral notices. "Whether legal or documented, they place her in the masculine," they write, describing a mismatch between her lived identity and formal recognition.
> 
> The complaint: "A system that does not represent everyone"
> The post raises a broader criticism: Alessandra's case is cited as an example of a system still tied to a binary approach that fails to represent all identities. "A sign that the law is still too tied to a binary system that doesn't represent everyone," the association writes.
> 
> A topic that recurs cyclically in public debate, especially in relation to the recognition of gender identity in official documents and at institutional events, such as those related to death. "Goodbye Ale, we will not forget you," concludes the ATN message.
