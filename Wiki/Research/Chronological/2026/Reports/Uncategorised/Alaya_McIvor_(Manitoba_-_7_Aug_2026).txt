> Community leader Alaya McIvor remembered as 'staunch advocate' for MMIWG2S+ families in Manitoba: minister
> Alaya McIvor organized many community events and vigils, created databases collecting families' stories
> Lauren Scott · CBC News · Posted: Aug 08, 2026 3:48 PM EDT | Last Updated: August 10
> 
> Alaya McIvor was an outspoken activist and family member who advocated on behalf of missing and murdered Indigenous women, girls and two-spirit people, and for missing and murdered Indigenous men and boys. (Submitted by Alaya McIvor)
>
> Alaya McIvor, a prominent Manitoba-based advocate for missing and murdered Indigenous women, girls and two-spirit individuals, is being remembered as a generous and passionate person who fought to make a difference for families that have lost loved ones. 
> 
> Indigenous leaders and community members from across the province and other parts of the country have been sharing heartfelt tributes online to McIvor, from Sandy Bay Ojibway First Nation, after she died earlier this week. 
> 
> Manitoba Families Minister Nahanni Fontaine, who is the minister responsible for women and gender equity, has considered McIvor a "sister" for more than two decades. 
> 
> "It's pretty disheartening news, devastating news that we lost such a staunch advocate and fighter and just really generous, loving person," Fontaine said of McIvor, who worked as a constituency assistant for the minister for several years.
> 
> CBC News confirmed McIvor's death with her family, who asked for privacy at this time. She would have turned 44 last Thursday.
> 
> A woman with short hair wears colourful beaded earrings and a beaded necklace while sitting in a group among other women
> Tributes highlighting Alaya McIvor's life and legacy have been pouring in online from across the province and other parts of the country. (Nahanni Fontaine/Facebook)
> Fontaine said she spoke with McIvor's mother on Friday. Support is being provided to the family through the Manitoba government's Matriarch Circle advisory group, of which McIvor was a member, and the provincial MMIWG2S+ endowment fund, the minister said.
> 
> As an outspoken family member of an MMIWG2S+ individual, McIvor organized and attended countless marches, vigils and events to honour lost loved ones.
> 
> "She was a staunch advocate for MMIWG families and to end violence against Indigenous women, girls and two-spirited [people]," Fontaine said, adding McIvor — who was two-spirit and transgender — always advocated for gender diverse people living in the province.
> 
> She spent years collecting information on missing and murdered Indigenous individuals and creating databases to share their stories. 
> 
> 'Our stories matter': Website honours lives of missing and murdered Indigenous women and girls across Manitoba
> 
> Her research helped to launch the Red Dress Stories MB website, which includes an interactive map highlighting the stories of more than 400 Indigenous women, girls, two-spirit and gender-diverse people who went missing or were found murdered in Manitoba. 
> 
> Her family has also been affected by the issue of missing and murdered Indigenous men and boys. She started documenting the names of MMIMB individuals in a database after she noticed that their stories weren't being heard.
> 
> McIvor shared her personal experience creating the MMIMB database in a first-person article for CBC Manitoba published a year ago. 
> 
> Fontaine said McIvor was a "fiercely protective" and funny person, who gave so much of herself to others.
> 
> "She was just very generous with her time, generous with the little that she did have and generous with her advocacy," the minister said, adding that McIvor always ensured family members and matriarchs were given a rose at every event she put on.
> 
> "She's going to be missed and I love her dearly," Fontaine said.
> 
> A tall woman with dark hair hugs a shorter woman with dark hair, who is wearing a festive Christmas hat
> Families Minister Nahanni Fontaine, right, says Alaya McIvor, left, was a passionate advocate and an incredibly generous person, who worked as the minister's constituency assistant in Winnipeg's St. Johns ward for several years. (Nahanni Fontaine/Facebook)
> Tributes to McIvor's life and legacy have been pouring out across social media over the past 24 hours. 
> 
> In a Friday night post on social media, Winnipeg Centre MP Leah Gazan wrote: "Sending condolences to the friends, family and community of Alaya McIvor. Rest in peace."
> 
> Bridget Tolley, founder of the grassroots group Families of Sisters in Spirit, said McIvor was part of a nationwide family of people affected by the MMIWG2S+ crisis. 
> 
> "Kichi Meegwetch for the truth you spoke, your strength, your determination to love and support all of our families no matter what," Tolley wrote in a social media post. 
> 
> First Person
> I was homeless, in despair, until 1 woman and team of 'heart helpers' saved me
> 
> First Person
> Missing and murdered Indigenous men and boys cases need more attention. That's why I started tracking them
> 
> Ka Ni Kanichihk, an Indigenous-led organization in Winnipeg, remembered McIvor as a "beloved warrior matriarch" who "walked this life with strength, courage, and a powerful voice" in a statement posted on Saturday. 
> 
> Melissa Robinson, co-founder of Morgan's Warriors, said in a post on Saturday morning that McIvor was a "fierce advocate" for MMIWG2S+ families who was a staple at local gatherings and events. 
> 
> "Her presence was known, her voice was heard, and her commitment to our families and our communities was undeniable. She leaves behind a space that cannot be filled," Robinson wrote. 
> 
> Diane Redsky said she and McIvor built a special friendship over many years of working together on various initiatives. She said McIvor was endlessly driven to make the world a safer place for Indigenous women, girls and gender-diverse people.
> 
> "She was really brave and courageous. She had a strong sense of purpose to do what is right, just speak for those who can't speak for themselves, to protect the most vulnerable," Redsky said.
> 
> "Alaya oftentimes was the one saying the things that nobody else had the courage to say."
> 
> McIvor loved her mother, family and First Nation deeply, Redsky said. And her two pets, Prince and Spooky, were right by her side all the time, her friend said.
> 
> 
> Redsky said her friend never gave up on the causes she cared for — and she'd want others to do the same.
> 
> She said McIvor would want community members to keep fighting for all 231 Calls for Justice of the National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls to be implemented. And she'd want events she started to continue in her honour, Redsky said.
> 
> "She leaves lots of seeds that she planted in the hearts and minds and spirits of so many people, young and old and from all different nations," Redsky said.
> 
> "Her legacy will live on."
