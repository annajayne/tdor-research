> Shock in Chuy over the death of a young trans woman: neighbors demanded justice and an investigation into the case
> Medical authorities point to a lung infection caused by bacteria. The demonstration marched through the city streets and culminated in front of the courthouse.
> June 7, 2026 - 9:26 PM
> 
> The death of Nicol Blanco, a 23-year-old trans woman , caused shock in Chuy and prompted a mobilization of neighbors and representatives of the LGBT community who demanded justice and an investigation to clarify the circumstances of her death.
> 
> The demonstration marched through the city streets and culminated in front of the local courthouse, where participants demanded transparency regarding the case and expressed doubts about how the events leading up to the young woman's death unfolded.
> 
> Michar Techera, a member of the LGBT collective in Chuy, pointed out that there are "contradictions between what has been said and what has been done" and demanded clarification to avoid confusion. "If there are guilty parties, well, let them pay," he stated during the demonstration.
> 
> Techera described Nicole as a leading figure in the local trans community and highlighted her involvement in activities related to defending the rights of the community. "She was a fighter and a warrior for her rights," she said.
> 
> For her part, the director of the Chuy Hospital, Yacqueline Almada, provided details about the medical care the young woman received before her death. She explained that Nicol visited the medical center four times due to discomfort and pain. During her initial consultations, she was seen by different doctors, received medication, and did not have a fever.
> 
> According to the official, the patient arrived at her last appointment in police custody after her mother requested assistance in getting her to the hospital. At that time, she was experiencing shortness of breath, and after tests revealed pulmonary congestion, she was transferred to the Rocha Hospital, where she was admitted to the Intensive Care Unit in serious condition.
> 
> Almada indicated that the diagnosis confirmed a pneumococcal infection, a bacterium that affected the young woman's lungs and which, she explained, can progress rapidly.
> 
> The director also noted that Nicole was undergoing hormone therapy that included corticosteroids, a medication that can cause immunosuppression and promote the progression of infections.
