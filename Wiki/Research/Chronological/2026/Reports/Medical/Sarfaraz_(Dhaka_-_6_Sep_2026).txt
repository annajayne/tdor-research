> A 31-year-old trans man from Gournadi, Barishal, died early Sunday, September 6, hours after undergoing gender-affirming surgery at a private hospital in Dhaka. The hospital registered him under the name Sarfaraz and identified him as male on his death certificate. He underwent surgery on Saturday afternoon and died around 4:30am the following morning. His family brought his body to Gournadi later that day and buried him that evening.
> 
> Family members told Samakal that Sarfaraz had expressed a masculine gender presentation since childhood and had been undergoing hormone therapy in Dhaka. 
> 
> His mother said he had never been interested in men and had previously been forced into marriage twice. Sarfaraz later met Jenifa Akter through social media, and the two developed a close relationship, with Jenifa regularly visiting his family home.
> 
> Jenifa said Sarfaraz’s parents provided Tk 150,000 for the surgery and that he was admitted under the name Sarfaraz on September 3. Jenifa signed the hospital consent form identifying herself as his wife. Sarfaraz’s father said he knew about plans to undergo surgery and later marry Jenifa but did not know 
> 
> Jenifa would sign the consent form or that the procedure would proceed on that basis. He said the family had entrusted Jenifa with arrangements for the surgery.
> 
> Questions remain about the circumstances surrounding Sarfaraz’s death and the medical care provided before, during and after the procedure. The report did not identify the specific operation performed or establish a medical cause of death. The hospital’s death certificate confirms his September 3 admission but provides no publicly reported explanation linking the surgery to his death.
> 
> When contacted, hospital administrator Limon referred questions to General Manager Bishwajit Baidya, who directed the reporter to the treating physician. Dr. Mahmudul Hasan, identified as the doctor involved in Sarfaraz’s care, ended the initial call saying he was busy and did not answer subsequent attempts. 
> 
> No detailed statement from the hospital or treating physician on the procedure or cause of death had been reported as of September 7.
> 
> Source: Samakal




> Rozi Akter Liza, 31, from Mahilara village in Gournadi, Barishal, died after undergoing surgery in Dhaka as part of a gender transition process. She died hours after the operation at Unihealth Specialized Hospital in Panthapath.
> 
> Liza’s death after surgery occurred early on September 6, following an operation performed on the afternoon of September 5. She had been admitted to the hospital on September 3 under the name ‘Sarfaraz’.
> 
> According to family members and local residents, Liza had preferred short hair and traditionally masculine clothing and behavior since childhood. Her mother said Liza had been married twice but did not continue either marriage.
> 
> Jenifa Akter, a close associate of Liza, said Liza had been receiving hormone therapy in Dhaka. The family reportedly provided Tk 150,000 for the surgery. Jenifa signed the surgical consent form identifying herself as Liza’s wife.
> 
> Liza’s father, freedom fighter Abdur Rab Hawlader, said he was unaware that she had been admitted under the name ‘Sarfaraz’ or that Jenifa had signed the consent form.
> 
> Liza’s mother, Fazila Begum, said the family had entrusted Jenifa with responsibilities related to the surgery. The hospital’s death certificate reportedly recorded Liza as being admitted under the male name ‘Sarfaraz’.
> 
> Hospital officials referred questions about the admission and treatment to the doctor responsible for admitting Liza. Attempts to obtain a detailed statement from the doctor were unsuccessful.
> 
> The exact medical cause of Liza’s death after surgery and whether any surgical complication occurred have not been independently confirmed. The hospital has not provided a detailed official statement on the matter.
> 
> The circumstances surrounding Liza’s death after surgery have raised questions about the admission process and treatment. Further clarification from the hospital and relevant medical authorities may be needed.
