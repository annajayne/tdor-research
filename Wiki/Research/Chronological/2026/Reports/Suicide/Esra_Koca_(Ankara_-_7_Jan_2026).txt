> In Ankara in Turkey, Esra Koca, a 40 yo trans woman, was found dead in her house on January 7, 2026. She had been dead for at least 15 days when she was found. She loved cooking. 
> 
> She was estranged from her parents who abandoned her at a young age, but her brother is now trying to get her justice. The police couldn't tell if it was suicide or a murder disguised in suicide.
> 
> The local queer activists and a few family members organised her funerals and her identity as a woman was respected.





> On the evening of January 7th, around 5 PM, while walking down Esat Street, we encountered a crowd of people and police towards the end of the street. A short distance further, a small group formed in front of Aspava, inquiring about what had happened. We learned from the Aspava waiters that someone had committed suicide and that the deceased was a transgender woman. After walking around for a while, we realized something needed to be done and returned to the scene. From the police, we learned that Esra had committed suicide by hanging herself and that her body had been hanging in the house for 15 days. We then contacted other activists and her brother. After a while, friends who had heard the news also arrived at the house. During this time, Esra's lifeless body, still in the house, was taken downstairs and placed in a hearse.
> 
> According to her friend, who was among the first to arrive at the scene, Esra wrote to her on the night of December 7th, saying she was going out, without specifying where she was going that night or the next day, and that she was leaving her vacuum cleaner and phone with her. After this message, she was never able to reach her again. It is unknown where and why Esra was going, especially since she had started a new life just a month earlier.
> 
> Esra was found hanging from the door frame at 4:30 PM on January 7th, exactly one month after this message. It is said she had been hanging there for 15 days. There was no stool or other support underneath, and the house key was left under the doormat outside the entrance. Almost all the windows were open. Birds had started entering and nesting. She was found after neighbors, suspicious of the situation, notified the police. Esra's phone was switched off and left charging. One neighbor said they had seen her four or five days before New Year's.
> 
> Then there's the boyfriend issue. Her friends say her boyfriend doesn't let her see anyone and doesn't want her to work. However, no one has ever met or seen this boyfriend.
> 
> Esra had used up all her money to buy a house and moved into a very old apartment building about to undergo urban transformation. All she owned was a broom and a television, both borrowed from a friend. Her neighbors, hairdresser, and friends described her as quiet, reserved, and rarely socializing. However, it was questionable whether this was a chosen solitude or if she had been driven into it.
> 
> This raises the question: If she really had a boyfriend, the one who wouldn't let her see anyone or work, where was he when she died? Why didn't he call her for fifteen days, or if he did, why didn't he get worried and check on her at home or inform the police when he couldn't reach her?
> 
> Esra may have been killed in an unsolved murder disguised as a suicide, or she may have genuinely taken her own life. We don't yet know which possibility is true. In today's conditions, where the state, through all its means, fuels hatred day after day, and trans poverty and insecurity are deepening at an increasing rate, there are many reasons to suspect that she was driven to suicide – if it was indeed a suicide. 
> 
> This death is, in any case, a murder, and this murder must be solved through a thorough and effective investigation.
> 
> Although Esra's parents didn't show much interest in the funeral, her brother and cousin were quite welcoming. Her brother stated that he had searched for her for years, trying to find her, and that if he had found her, he would have accepted her in every way. According to her brother's account, Esra was left at a Children's Protection Institution in Samsun because of her feminine nature, and because of the fear of gossip from relatives and neighbors, who were ashamed of her. Her brother was also left there. A painting she made while at the institution was submitted to a competition and won first prize. A newspaper of the time featured an interview with her about her experiences at the institution, along with news of her artistic achievements. Her brother stated that he kept the painting and the newspaper article. This was her first and only media coverage, apart from LGBT+ press coverage of her suspicious death and funeral.
> 
> Her brother agrees with activists and lawyers that her death was suspicious. He has testified that the death was suspicious and is cooperating with Pink Life and Kaos GL lawyers to ensure an investigation is conducted. Furthermore, a very welcome development for all of us is the decision, reached through a consensus among activists and family members, that Esra will be laid to rest in Ankara alongside her comrades. Moreover, her tombstone will bear her own name, not her assigned name: Esra Koca.
> 
>  If we hadn't met her there that day, we might not even have known she was transgender. The fact that, at the request of the families, her coffin was moved from the men's section to the women's section, and that she was referred to as Esra Koca, the deceased, during the funeral prayer, were extraordinary achievements. As a result of this encounter and the subsequent mobilization, we sent her off at the young age of 40, surrounded by a crowd of comrades, fellow activists, LGBT+ families, her brother, and her niece, embracing her existence and using her own name. Throughout the entire process, activists from Pembe Hayat, Lambdaistanbul, Kaos GL, and Galader demonstrated incredible organization and solidarity, honoring her struggle.
> 
> According to her friends, Esra was a wonderful cook. She was a very skilled cook, kind-hearted, well-intentioned, resilient, and compassionate.
> 
> Let's hope that if this is a murder, it will be solved and the culprit(s) punished. If she left us by suicide, let's hope the reasons that drove her to suicide are thoroughly investigated. We will be following the case and will never leave her alone again.
