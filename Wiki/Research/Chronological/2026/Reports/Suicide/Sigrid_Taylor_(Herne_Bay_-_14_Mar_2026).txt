> Herne Bay mum says death of daughter, 19, highlights ‘emotional toll and social isolation faced by trans people’
> Published: 05:00, 01 July 2026
> 
> The death of a much-loved 19-year-old highlights the “emotional toll and social isolation” faced by transgender people, her heartbroken mum says.
> 
> The family of Sigrid Taylor, who lived in Herne Bay, say she took her own life after struggling with her mental health.
> 
> Her mum, Penny, says she is heartbroken by the loss of her “beautiful, compassionate, deeply authentic” daughter.
> 
> She told KentOnline that Sigrid “came out relatively recently” and was “doing very well”, having made “a good number of acquaintances in the trans world”.
> 
> In an emotional tribute, she described her daughter as a kind and caring young woman who had a natural understanding of other people.
> 
> Sigrid was born in Ashford and raised in Canterbury, attending St Peter's Methodist Primary until the age of seven, when the Taylor family relocated to the USA.
> 
> She moved back to Kent, this time settling in Herne Bay with her mum and her sister Ruby, about a year before her death.
> 
> Sigrid soon found her feet in the town, securing work in an admin role and becoming a hugely valued colleague and friend to many.
> 
> “She had an independent life - she wanted to find her own way,” Penny said.
> 
> “She was quite confident in her gentle way. She wasn't a loud person, she was never mean or cruel and she loved being with friends and helping others.”
> 
> Penny painted a picture of a sociable young woman who loved to sing, dance and act.
> 
> Sigrid loved history and madrigals - poems set to music. She had a fondness for cats and dragons and enjoyed dressing up.
> 
> She also loved Costa Rica, New York and big cities, and was described as fashionable, creative and deeply curious about the world.
> 
> In a fundraiser recently launched to help pay for a gravestone, Penny called Sigrid’s death “a tragedy highlighting the profound emotional toll and social isolation faced by trans people”.
> 
> She noted her daughter had sought help from the NHS multiple times for her mental health and for support linked to her transition before her tragic death in March.
> 
> Writing on the GoFundMe page, she said her daughter’s short life reflected the need for “empathy, support and safe spaces” for the trans community.
> 
> “Forever loved, Sigrid leaves a dear sister Ruby, her parents, and dogs. We are broken-hearted by the loss,” she added.
