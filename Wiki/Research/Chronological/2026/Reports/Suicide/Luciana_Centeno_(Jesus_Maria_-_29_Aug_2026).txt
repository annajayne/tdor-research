> Luciana Centeno was 40 years old and an activist with the Association of Transvestites and Transgender People of Argentina (ATTA) in Jesús María. For years she was at the forefront of the fight to defend the transvestite and transgender community in Córdoba.
> 
> This Saturday, Luciana died, but we cannot reduce her death to an individual case of suicide (which are on the rise in Argentina ). We must remember that days before, Luciana had gone through a crisis that ended up being publicly exposed. The images and comments that circulated around that episode didn't just show a person who needed support: in various places, her suffering was turned into an object of ridicule and public exposure, not only by random people, but also by local journalists who didn't recognize her and joined in the wave of mockery. Given this, it's worth asking what responsibility those who decided to spread and ridicule those images bear, and above all, what support was available for her.
> 
> Therefore, talking about Luciana requires more than simply reconstructing the circumstances of her death. It requires discussing a community that has been denouncing abandonment, violence, and a lack of support resources. And it requires discussing the responsibility of the State and governments in the face of a reality that neither began with Luciana nor ends with her.
> 
> That's why what happened in Jesús María requires deeper analysis, because, particularly in the trans community, life expectancy is no more than 40 years. Limited access to employment, a life of poverty, exclusion from their own homes from a young age, not to mention discrimination, violence, and all forms of mental abuse, are the conditions that lead to this alarming mortality rate.
> 
> All these conditions lead to prostitution being the last resort for survival in this society. This is a clear reflection of state neglect and responsibility.
> 
> Given this, it is necessary to name and identify those responsible for the community's current situation.
> 
> It is necessary to denounce Miguel Ángel Siciliano, who is the Minister of Institutional Relations and Management, whose position appears invisible in this case (and many more).
> 
> But one of the main people responsible for this is the aggressor Franco Bonino, the director of Sexual Diversities of the municipality, who was denounced since 2023 by Virginia Digón and Matías Calderón for having harassing attitudes mainly towards trans people and also for embezzlement of funds.
> 
> In addition, a trans woman filed a report with the Santa Fe Women's Center, alleging human trafficking. The report accuses Bonino of "unlawful deprivation of liberty" for keeping her locked up in his home for two months. The report also states that the trans woman was "forced to buy drugs" and "to solicit financial assistance in his name."
> 
> But we mustn't forget the leader of the ruling party, Martín Llaryora, who is the top implementer of austerity measures in the province of Córdoba. Furthermore, his staff is riddled with murderers, femicide perpetrators, drug traffickers, and several individuals accused of human trafficking. Let's not forget what happened just a few weeks ago with the ruling party legislator Ernesto Ramón "Cañito" Flores , who is linked to those arrested for operating a child sex trafficking ring in the town of Villa María de Río Seco, in the northern part of the province. And let's certainly not forget the case of Agostina Vega, whose murderer was a member of the ruling party.
> 
> In response, the trans community is no longer waiting: it demands an investigation into who disseminated the videos, the journalists involved, and so on. But it also demands justice for the officials responsible for the trans community's suffering. They are all completely complicit in the reactionary policies of Javier Milei's government, a government known for its attacks primarily against the LGBTIQ+ community and women.
> 
> That is why the community, now more than ever, must organize alongside the working class to confront these reactionary policies of adjustment and social exclusion imposed by all governments.



> IT WAS A SOCIAL TRAVESTICIDE
> (Two days ago, in Jesús María, Córdoba, Luciana Centeno—a 40-year-old trans woman and activist with the Argentine Association of Travestis and Transsexuals—took her own life.)
> 
> She was supported by local *travesti* and trans community organizations—groups that did what we *travestis* always do for one another: holding each other up with whatever is available—emotional support, presence, and our own limited resources—precisely because the State is nowhere to be found. Because that is what this is about: not a partial absence, but a structural, long-standing absence that serves to ensure we continue to die prematurely.
> I am not writing this simply to report the news. I am writing this to point—naming those responsible—to what society stubbornly refuses to hear: when a *travesti* or trans person commits suicide, it is not an isolated incident. It is a preventable death. There is nothing inevitable about it. What exists is an absent State.
> 
> We *travestis* are kicked out of our homes while still children or adolescents. That is not a cliché; it is a statistic, a recurring history, the reality for so many of us—and the reality Luciana faced until the very end. After life on the streets comes workplace marginalization, which pushes the majority of us into sex work as our only means of survival, since the State fails to guarantee real access to formal employment—even when quota laws exist that the State ought to enforce but doesn't.
> Mental health protocols for people in situations of critical vulnerability exist on paper, yet they were never designed with us in mind; they were designed to pathologize, not to support us. In practice, activating them relies on the goodwill of community organizations working without a single cent of public funding.
> 
> It is a social *travesticide*—a way of killing us that requires no weapon, relying instead on apathy, a lack of policy, and the normalization of the idea that early death is simply to be expected for a *travesti* or trans person.
> We do not want a State that offers condolences. We demand a State that prevents. We will not allow Luciana’s name to be forgotten.
> 
> By Florencia Guimarae

> Cordoba: Trans woman found dead on the side of Route 9 in Jesus Maria
> 
> Drivers passing through the area spotted the body lying on the side of the road and immediately notified the authorities. Upon arriving at the scene, police officers confirmed the death of the  40-year-old transgender woman and secured the area. The victim's identity has not yet been officially released.
> 
> The discovery was made in the  early hours of the morning in the jurisdiction of Jesús María, Córdoba province.  Judicial Police personnel quickly arrived at the scene to begin the corresponding forensic work on  National Route 9. The officers collected evidence at the site to determine the circumstances surrounding the death.
> 
> Judicial authorities indicated that no hypothesis regarding  the cause of death has been ruled out . The prosecutor's office immediately intervened in the case to coordinate the relevant procedures. An  autopsy is pending to determine the cause of death.
> 
> The local community remains shocked by the tragic discovery beside the  main road . The investigation is ongoing, and  law enforcement is keeping sensitive information about the operation confidential. Further official information is expected in the coming hours to help clarify this unfortunate incident.
> 
> They analyze background information and recent videos.
> 
> In recent hours, it has emerged that investigators will analyze possible  prior episodes of crisis the victim may have experienced in public. According to reports circulating in the area, the woman had been involved in an altercation in the street days earlier and was restrained by  police . Experts are seeking to determine if there was a pre-existing  vulnerability or mental health condition that contributed to the outcome.
> 
> If this hypothesis is confirmed, the court will also evaluate the  protocol followed during the previous intervention. Authorities are trying to determine whether the person received  appropriate medical attention after that incident. The investigation remains strictly confidential pending official confirmation.
