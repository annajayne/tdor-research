> Mom urges acceptance and support for trans youth after son's death
> Kaden Hammond, 15, is remembered as a kid who cared about others and wanted other trans kids to be supported
> Oct 8, 2026 8:59 PM
> 
> ELORA - The mother of an Elora teen who died by suicide is speaking out about the need for stronger supports for trans kids in the community.
> 
> “If you’re a parent and you have a queer kid, you constantly have to worry,” says Angela Hammond.
> 
> Her oldest child, Kaden Hammond, was an exceptional artist, who loved video games, nature and animals, and had strong relationships with both family and close friends.
> 
> But he also struggled with anxiety and mental health issues and experienced bullying in school.
> 
> “He struggled with feeling he couldn’t be his authentic self in front of people,” Angela says.
> 
> The 15-year-old died by suicide Sept. 27, a fact the family shared candidly in his obituary, along with a message about the importance of supporting 2SLGBTQIA+ youth and creating safe spaces where young people feel accepted.
> 
> “Kaden deserved a world that fully saw, celebrated and protected him,” the obituary reads. “In his memory, we ask that you fight fiercely for the acceptance, safety and dignity of all transgender people.”
> 
> At birth, Kaden was assigned female at birth, but later questioned that label.
> 
> His mom says he wasn’t a fan of they/them pronouns and eventually settled on he/him.
> 
> But he faced scrutiny from the community for doing things like getting his nails done or wearing a skirt.
> 
> “Gender is a journey, and all kids are allowed to have that journey,” says Angela, but she also noted that in rural communities there can be a lack of opportunities for safe self-expression.
> 
> “He felt ostracized by a lot of people,” she says.
> 
> At the same time, Kaden was accepted and well-supported at home, loved by his younger siblings, Kai, 12 and Finna, 9, and both his parents.
> 
> “We supported him as much as possible. He knew he was loved,” says Angela.
> 
> At home, he had pretty good self-esteem, she says, noting how he helped around the house and helped care for his younger siblings.
> 
> School wasn’t as easy, with Kaden struggling with the post-pandemic return to the classroom.
> 
> Kaden attended Trillium Waldorf for a time and later attempted to attend Elora Public School, but that only lasted a couple of weeks before he was bullied.
> 
> “He quickly didn’t want to be at school,” Angela says.
> 
> She says school staff had seemed supportive, but didn’t always ensure Kaden felt safe.
> 
> He was able to get a counsellor through the Canadian Mental Health Association after a suicide attempt two and a half years ago, but that also wasn’t a successful experience.
> 
> “The biggest thing for me is the fact that the system kind of failed,” says Angela.
> 
> Accommodations that would have benefited Kaden – like being able to have counselling sessions at home, so he wouldn’t have to go to a public building for appointments – were not available, she says.
> 
> There’s a general lack of funding and support for mental health, and in Kaden’s case, there were barriers to getting him the support he needed, she says.
> 
> Though still not a perfect fit, high school was somewhat better for Kaden.
> 
> He applied and “got in, no problem,” to the integrated arts program at Kitchener’s Eastwood Collegiate Institute, and the arts community was generally more welcoming, says Angela.
> 
> “It’s a very inclusive space,” she says, but she acknowledged he was again not feeling comfortable at school.
> 
> He seemed to get along with the adults, Angela says, noting teachers reached out to her to tell her how awesome Kaden was.
> 
> “He was like an adult that just needed to get past the awkward teenage times,” she says.
> 
> He spent a lot of time with his best friend, Jade, another queer kid he had known since third grade, and had a far-reaching community of friends he met online.
> 
> Kaden cared about helping others, and it was important to him to ensure other transgender youth were supported, Angela says.
> 
> He also loved nature, spending lots of time sitting by the river watching the birds and animals.
> 
> He enjoyed a variety of artistic pursuits, taking up needle felting, and going through a phase of making puppets and masks that he would bring to life in TikTok or YouTube videos.
> 
> He spent hours drawing and animating his characters, and always had a sketchbook by his side.
> 
> “He’d bring his notebook wherever he went and just doodle,” says Angela.
> 
> She and her husband Michael were working on printing out photos of Kaden’s art, as well as dozens of photos and selfies of him to share at his celebration of life on Saturday.
> 
> “He’s a teenager, and constantly taking selfies. There’s so many pictures, so it’s hard to choose,” Angela says.
> 
> She noted that Kaden might not approve of the attention, but he would value helping others deal with their loss.
> 
> “He did say he doesn’t want others to struggle,” she says.
> 
> In his memory, the Hammond family is asking that people make donations to CW Pride or Art Not Shame, two organizations that embody things that were significant to Kaden.
> 
> Angela has been involved with CW Pride since it started, and helped launch the Art Your Way program, which Kaden was supposed to have volunteered with.
> 
> “As soon as they asked for community volunteers . . . I went right away,” says Angela.
> 
> “We need to do more for the queer community,” she says, noting very little exists in rural communities to support queer youth.
> 
> Angela’s priority is CW Pride, as she feels the need is greater in Centre Wellington.
> 
> She hopes to be able to use donations to create a program in Kaden’s memory to bring the community together to support youth like him.
> 
> In the midst of her loss, she wants to remind others to let their kids be themselves and to teach their children that everybody has value.
> 
> “I urge people to hold their kids, and not judge their kids for being queer,” she says.
