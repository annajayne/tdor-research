> Inman Cluff In Loving Memory
> 
> August 17, 2005-June 13, 2026
> 
> Inman came into the world fighting. He struggled to breathe at birth, going five minutes without a breath and spending about a week in the NICU a hard beginning that shaped the rest of his life.
> 
> He grew into a particular child, one with some of the longest eyelashes and the widest grin you ever saw. As a small child, he could light up a room just by walking into it though he wanted no part of finger paint, and no one had better touch his hands, his lips, or his nose. His teachers at Mother's Day Out adored him, and so did his teachers at school, even as school itself tested him. He was great at fractions and hopeless at addition and subtraction, but he came alive in history, especially the First and Second World Wars. He became an avid reader, lost in the Warriors series, and he was a Brony through and through, with My Little Pony never far from the screen. When he finally walked down that graduation aisle, grinning ear to ear, the struggle was behind him and a hard-won goal was his.
> 
> From there, his confidence only grew. When a family friend opened a horseback riding school, Inman and his brother Nolan started lessons and it became a turning point. Learning to handle and ride those horses built him up in the very places he'd always struggled, and he began to see that he could do hard things on his own. That same growth carried into his stepmother and father's restaurant, where working with people brought out a confidence no one expected. He could juggle customers and phone calls at the same time a skill that stumped plenty of people, his own father included.
> 
> Then came the motors. Inman ordered a model V8 engine and built it himself. The little boy who once watched Cars over and over had grown into a young man who loved toy cars, then real ones and at eighteen, with his license in hand, that love became a love of driving. Near the end of his life, he was dreaming of the road ahead: becoming a truck driver, or a mechanic.
> 
> Inman was a victor, and every struggle he turned into a victory.
