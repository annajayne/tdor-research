> Juneteenth memorial planned for transgender woman who died in the Birmingham City Jail
> Published: Jun. 17, 2026, 2:45 p.m.
> 
> Birmingham’s TAKE Resource Center, a transgender advocacy organization, will be hosting a balloon sendoff memorial event Saturday to honor the life of a transgender woman who died at the city jail.
> 
> Kalena “Peaches” Croskey, 32, was found unresponsive during a routine check last Thursday afternoon, according to Deputy Jefferson County Coroner Matt Angelo.
> 
> Jail staff attempted to resuscitate her and called for medical assistance, but she was declared dead at the scene.
> 
> Birmingham Deputy Chief of Public Safety Carlton Peeples said Croskey was booked into the jail on April 21 on charges of disorderly conduct, public intoxication, criminal mischief, and two counts of third-degree assault.
> 
> She had been housed in the Birmingham City Jail for about 51 days prior to her death.
> 
> Peeples said Croskey was unresponsive from a “self-harm incident” in a statement Monday but did not elaborate further.
> 
> TAKE Resource Center, called Croskey’s death “not accidental” in a public statement.
> 
> “It is a tragic reminder of the failures within the carceral system,” the group stated.
> 
> “The lack of adequate mental health resources, combined with negligence and systemic shortcomings in our system, continues to cost lives.”
> 
> Police have said Croskey’s death and the death of another jail inmate three days later both stemmed from a “self-harm incident.”
> 
> The balloon release to honor Croskey will take place June 20, at 2:30 p.m. at 7128 Oporto Madrid Boulevard South in Birmingham.
> 
> “We will pause to commemorate Peaches with a few tender words and a special balloon release in her honor,” reads a Facebook post from TAKE.
> 
> “We ask everyone to bring a white or gold balloon as we send her spirit off with love.”
> 
> Police are investigating in coordination with the Jefferson County Coroner’s Office, which will determine the official cause and manner of death in each case, according to a previous statement.




> The transgender community in Birmingham, Alabama is mourning the loss of Kalena “Peaches” Croskey, a 32-year-old Black trans woman who died last Thursday while being held in city jail.
> 
> Initially misgendered in local media reports, the nonprofit TAKE Resource Center wrote on Substack this week that Croskey was “more than a community member.”
> 
> “She was family,” the organization, which focuses on providing social services to trans people of color, wrote in a June 15 post. “She brought light, laughter, and joy into every space she entered. Despite the many challenges she faced throughout her life, she never allowed those struggles to define her. Whether she was singing her favorite Beyoncé songs, voguing to the beat, creating glamorous makeup looks, or simply making others smile, Peaches found ways to experience joy and share it with those around her.”
> 
> Early coverage of Croskey’s death only identified her using her birth name and referred to her as a man. Local NBC affiliate WVTM cited information provided by the Jefferson County Coroner’s Office stating that Croskey was found unresponsive during a routine check at Birmingham City Jail and could not be resuscitated. Per WVTM, Croskey had been in the jail since April 21 on charges of disorderly conduct, public intoxication, criminal mischief, and third-degree assault.
> 
> Three days after Croskey’s death, another person died in Birmingham City Jail shortly after being evaluated for a self-harm attempt. Both deaths are under investigation by the Birmingham Police Department, according to WVTM, which cited its own reporting on deteriorating conditions inside the facility over the last several years.
> 
> “This loss is not accidental. It is a tragic reminder of the failures within the carceral system. The lack of adequate mental health resources, combined with negligence and systemic shortcomings in our system, continues to cost lives,” TAKE Resource Center wrote in its Substack post. “Far too many people are dying at the hands of systems that cause harm rather than healing. Incarceration should never lead to dehumanization. Peaches was a human being. She deserved dignity, respect, compassion, and access to the care she needed.”
> 
> In a separate Facebook post, TAKE Resource Center founder and executive director Daroneshia Duncan-Boyd referred to Croskey’s death as a failure of the criminal justice system, writing a heartfelt tribute to her departed friend.
> 
> “Despite living with untreated mental illness and having to navigate this world alone in your trans identity, you still managed to bring so many people joy,” Duncan-Boyd wrote. “The love you had for me is something I can’t fully put into words. Once again, a system has failed you.”
> 
> Her post also contained alleged details of Croskey’s time in the jail based on Duncan-Boyd’s communications with her. “I think about the bullying you endured when you first got there Birmingham City Jail,” the nonprofit leader wrote. “I think about the lack of access to specialty medications, the failure to prioritize your medical appointments, the disgusting food you spoke about, the pest infestations, the broken air conditioning, and the busted windows throughout the facility.”
> 
> In 2025, Birmingham City Jail was hit with a federal lawsuit on behalf of an inmate, Angela Karen Langley Kimberly, who allegedly died of COVID-19 in her cell, as AL.com reported. “In my experience going down to the jail to see clients, you can see that it is in deplorable condition,” attorney Jonathan Austin told the outlet at the time. “This is not unique to this one case.”
> 
> Attorneys said that Kimberly had been left unmedicated and “struggling to breathe” for ten days after testing positive for the virus before she died.
> 
> TAKE Resource Center is planning a short memorial and a balloon release for Croskey at its forthcoming Juneteenth event on the 20th.
> 
> “Rest peacefully, Peaches,” the nonprofit wrote. “Your life mattered, your presence was felt, and your memory will forever remain with us.”
