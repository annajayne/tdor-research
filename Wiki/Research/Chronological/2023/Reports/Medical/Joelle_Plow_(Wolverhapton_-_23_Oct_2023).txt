> Trans Legal Clinic are calling for justice for a trans cancer patient 
> BY ELLA GAUCI, IMAGE VIA TRANS LEGAL CLINIC 
> Trans Legal Clinic is calling for justice for Joelle, a 27-year-old trans woman who died after a delay in finding her a bed for urgent cancer treatment in 2023.  Trans Legal Clinic is the first grassroots, trans-led specialist human rights and legal practice in the UK.
> 
> On its website, Joelle’s mother has published a powerful statement about the treatment of her daughter at Wolverhampton NHS Trust. Joelle had an aggressive form of lymphoma, which is “almost entirely curable when treated immediately, but it is rapidly and certainly fatal when treatment is delayed”.
> 
> Joelle’s mother states that there was an available bed on the cancer ward, but Joelle was instead placed in a single room on an inappropriate ward. “Joelle was placed there because she was trans,” her mother continued. “She was refused the available bed on the cancer ward because it was in a multi-bed women’s ward.” 
> 
> “Joelle needed treatment immediately. A delay of 16 days was impossible to survive and cost her her life.” 
> 
> Due to the nature of Joelle’s lymphoma, Trans Legal Clinic writes that “she explicitly pleaded for a bed on either the male or female ward”. However, Trans Legal Clinic states that “because she was trans they made her wait for a rare single room”. Joelle was told that emergency chemotherapy could not start until she was in the right ward. 
> 
> This event did not occur in a vacuum. Joelle’s mother writes that just the day before Joelle was refused a bed, Conservative health minister Steve Barclay had announced plans to ban trans women from women’s wards in hospitals. 
> 
> Joelle is remembered as “precious, beloved and magical”. She was a musician who achieved a first-class degree from a conservatoire. She taught herself to code and made a career in technology. 
> 
> ​Her mother continued: “In the care of the NHS, Joelle suffered intense physical pain, akin to torture, and the mental agony of knowing that she needed emergency treatment while we tried desperately to secure it. Daily transphobia became our lived reality. Neither Joelle’s suffering nor her death was unavoidable. We cannot shrug this off as a mishap.” 
> 
> With the Trans Legal Clinic, Joelle’s family are looking for accountability for the decision to deny Joelle a bed in the cancer ward and the 16-day delay in her treatment. Her mother also hopes that an inquest into the systemic failures that led to Joelle’s death will mean that no trans person will have to face the same harm. 
> 
> Caroline Litman, the mother of a trans woman named Alice who died by suicide, commented on Trans Legal Clinic’s Instagram post about Joelle. “As a doctor I can confirm that Joelle’s death is an entirely avoidable tragedy. The barriers that her mum faced to getting her treatment for her non-trans related medical illness are disgraceful and the barriers put in her path as she both grieves and tries to pursue justice for Joelle and ongoing safety in healthcare and society for all trans people, is a classic example of how little those in power care. In their desperate attempt to maintain supremacy they are prepared to retraumatise anyone who has lived experience of the harms their anti-trans policies inflict.” 



> MESSAGE FROM JOELLE'S MUM:
> Dear friends,
> 
> My daughter Joelle was 27 when she died after Wolverhampton NHS Trust took 16 days to find her a bed for urgent cancer treatment.
> 
> Joelle had an aggressive form of lymphoma that affects children and young people in their twenties. It has become almost entirely curable when treated immediately, but it is rapidly and certainly fatal when treatment is delayed. In its urgency and survival rate, it is closer to appendicitis than to most cancers. The treatment pathway is well understood. Joelle needed treatment immediately. A delay of 16 days was impossible to survive and cost her her life.
> 
> There was a bed available on the cancer ward on the day Joelle was admitted. I know because we saw it. She was initially taken to that ward. When staff saw her, they changed their minds. Joelle was taken back to A&E for 12 hours and then placed alone in a single room on an inappropriate ward on the far side of the hospital.
> 
> Joelle was placed there because she was trans. She was refused the available bed on the cancer ward because it was in a multi-bed women’s ward.
>  
> The day before, Conservative health minister Steve Barclay had announced plans to block trans women from women’s wards, saying: “As Conservatives we know what a woman is.”
> 
> Being placed on the wrong ward did more than delay Joelle’s cancer treatment. It left her in the care of staff who had no experience or competence in cancer pain management and did not understand how rapidly someone with aggressive lymphoma could deteriorate.
> 
> Joelle spent 16 days in intense, persistent and unmanaged pain. Her abdominal tumour measured 98cm. Her normal weight was eight stone. Her deterioration was expected for this form of lymphoma, but the steps needed to protect and care for her were not taken.
> 
> Joelle is dead because she was trans.
> 
> Joelle was precious, beloved and magical. She had an inventive, agile mind. She was generous, articulate and funny, with integrity and courage. She was a gifted musician who achieved a first from a conservatoire. She also taught herself to code and built a career in technology. She was deeply loved by her family, her friends and her partner.
> 
> In the care of the NHS, Joelle suffered intense physical pain, akin to torture, and the mental agony of knowing that she needed emergency treatment while we tried desperately to secure it. Daily transphobia became our lived reality.
> 
> Neither Joelle’s suffering nor her death was unavoidable. We cannot shrug this off as a mishap.
> 
> We want accountability for the decision to deny her the available cancer-ward bed, the 16-day delay in treatment, the failure to manage her pain and the failure to respond appropriately as her condition deteriorated.
>  
> The second reason reaches beyond Joelle’s individual case. An Article 2 inquest can examine the systemic failures that contributed to her death and help protect other people from the same harm.
> 
> The Supreme Court ruling against trans people in April 2025 is widely discussed in relation to toilets. Its effect on trans people’s access to hospital beds has received far less attention. If trans people are blocked from accessing appropriate hospital wards, what happened to Joelle could become a matter of policy across the country.
>  
> 
> People may imagine that, in an emergency, no young person would be left to die in favour of an ideological policy. I am here as a witness to say otherwise. This is exactly what happened to my child.
> 
> Joelle was left to suffer, plead for her life and die a horrific death at 27.
>  
> The Trans Legal Clinic is helping us fight for an inquest and pursue a clinical negligence claim. Your donation will help us seek justice for Joelle and help ensure that no other trans person is denied urgent medical care because of who they are.
> 
> Please donate, share Joelle’s story and help us demand accountability.
