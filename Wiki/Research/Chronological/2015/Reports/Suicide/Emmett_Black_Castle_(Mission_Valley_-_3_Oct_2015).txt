> Born: Apr 12, 2001 in San Clemente, CA
> 
> Died: Oct 03, 2015 in San Diego, CA
> 
> Emmett Castle was a brilliant young man who continues to be loved and treasured by his parents, his extended family and close friends. Those lucky enough to get to know him well were blessed by his sense of humor, his intelligence and kindness. He loved to help people and truly cared about others.
> 
> Talented in many art forms (painting, writing, music), his writing ability was a big part of his life and he enjoyed sharing this through different mediums. He especially enjoyed online blogging where he could discuss both subject and writing styles with others.
> 
> Although they were not his first love, Emmett was advanced in math and sciences. He enjoyed knowing he was close to surpassing his dad's math abilities, and made sure everyone knew it!
> 
> Emmett often appeared shy but upon speaking with him for any length of time it became apparent that it was not shyness. Rather, he thought before speaking as he was conscious of others' feelings and thoughts. Emmett understood that words have impact. 
> 
> Emmett will be greatly missed by everyone who knew him. To celebrate his life let's reflect on how to become kinder to one another. To not only accept others for who they are, but also celebrate those things that make us unique.
> 
Emmett Black Castle died at his home October 3, 2015 in San Diego, CA and will always be a beloved son, grandson, nephew, cousin and friend. He was enrolled as a freshman at Kearny High - School of Science, Connections and Technology. 
> 
> Born on April 12, 2001, at Camp Pendleton Naval Hospital, Camp Pendleton, CA, his parents quickly realized his affinity for books and writing. As an infant, he would cry when his bed time story was over and would only be consoled when it was read again from the beginning. By the age of 5, Emmett was reading Huckleberry Finn and writing complex stories. At the time of his death, he was planning on tutoring elementary school children at the local library.
> 
> Emmett loved animals. He saved a baby Cooper's Hawk who had fallen out of it's nest in Mission Hills as well as a stray cat. Emmett volunteered his time at a local cat rescue and would donate his spare change to the rescue. Animals, especially cats, were often characters in his stories and drawings. One of his favorite places to be was in trees. He was often found climbing the tree in his Mission Hills home or his favorite one in Pioneer Park. 
> 
> While he didn't often break out in laughter, his smile was something to be treasured. After getting to know Emmett, people often commented on how kind and respectful he was and enjoyed his sense of humor.
> 
> Emmett will always be remembered for being a brave soul. 
> 
> He is survived by his parents, Mike Castle and Sarah Castle, both of San Diego, his paternal grandmother, Sandi Castle of Colorado, his maternal grandmother Pamela Black of Washington, DC, his maternal grandfather Clifford Black of Minnesota as well as aunts, uncles and cousins who loved him. He is predeceased by his paternal grandfather, John Castle. 
> 
> A celebration of Emmett's life will be held at Pioneer Park in Missions Hills, San Diego, CA on October 24, 2015 from 2-4pm. All are welcome. The family requests that in lieu of flowers donations be sent to The LGBT Center in Hillcrest, San Diego, CA at 3909 Centre Street, San Diego, CA 92103. Their website is https://www.thecentersd.org/contact/.
> 
> Obituary from Legacy.com



> San Diego Mourns Fourth Trans Teen Lost to Suicide This Year
> 
> Beloved by his mother and father, Sarah and Mike Castle, and friends and family, Emmett Castle, a sensitive, quietly mature 14-year-old transgender boy in San Diego took his life Saturday morning at his father's Mission Valley home, according to local news station KGTV.
> 
> "He had a wisdom beyond his years" and a big heart, his mother told KGTV.
> 
> Castle began transitioning last summer, and his family immediately accepted him, encouraging him to attend meetings at the Hillcrest Youth Center at the San Diego LGBT Community Center. The Hillcrest Youth Center is the only drop-in and recreational center in San Diego County dedicated to the needs of LGBT youth.
> 
> His family sought medical help for Castle's severe depression, but despite these interventions, Castle had previously attempted to take his life several times, his mother shared with to KGTV. She emphasized that there were no immediate warning signs that would have prepared the family for what happened Saturday morning, noting that the teen was excited about legally changing his name later in the month.
> 
> Yet his mother reported that the physical changes of puberty were difficult for Castle, and his occasional misgendering at school upset him to the point where the teen would ask to be picked up by his father.
> 
> This is the fourth trans teen reportedly passing by suicide in the San Diego area this year. The Advocate last reported on the death of Kyler Prescott, another 14-year-old transgender boy, who was an accomplished pianist and activist for marriage equality and animal rights. Prescott, who died by suicide on May 18, was beloved by his family who affirmed him through his transition. Prescott and his's family also attended local San Diego support groups for family members of trans youth. Their support mirrors the love shown to Castle by his family.
> 
> Taylor Alesana, a 16-year-old transgender girl near San Diego, took her life on April 2, while a gender-nonconforming teenager named Sage David Quigley also died by suicide in early March. Both Alesana and Sage were reportedly involved with the North County LGBTQ Resource Center, where Prescott also attended various group meetings.
> 
> With the Castle family's blessing, the young man's aunt, Danielle Stickler, and family friend Allison Erickson, have set up the Emmett Castle Memorial fund as a GoFundMe page where the public may contribute funds towards funeral costs, with remaining donations to be given directly to the the San Diego LGBT Community Center.
> 
> In her discussion with KGTV, Castle's mother urged family members of trans youth to love their child and seek help when it is needed.
> 
> To trans teens themselves, the grieving mother has a more urgent message:
> 
> "I just want them to hold on. The transition can be painful, but we love you. We love you."
> 
> From Advocate.com


> Following his passing, many people who knew him posted tributes on social media using the hashtags #420SageIt and #HisNameWasSage. Sage was the first of four reported suicides of transgender teens in San Diego County in 2015, followed by [Taylor Alesana](https://tdor.translivesmatter.info/reports/2015/04/02/taylor-alesana_fallbrook-california-usa_3405c583), Kyler Cade Prescott, and Emmett Black Castle.
