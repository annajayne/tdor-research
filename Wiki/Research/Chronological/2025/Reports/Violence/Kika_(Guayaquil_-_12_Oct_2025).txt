> PRESS RELEASE
> : STOP THE VIOLENCE! Silueta X condemns the massacre in Guayaquil and demands justice for the transfemicide of Kika, one of its victims
>  Asociación Silueta X, an organization dedicated to defending the rights of LGBTIQ+ people and an active member of the Ecuadorian Federation of LGBT+ Organizations, expresses its deepest condemnation of the chilling massacre that occurred on the night of Sunday, October 12, on 22nd Street and Cristóbal Colón, in southwest Guayaquil.
> 
> This brutal attack, which left six people dead and at least 15 injured – including two teenagers aged 14 and 16, and a six-year-old child – adds to the wave of violence that has hit our city during the Independence Day holiday, becoming the third massacre recorded in recent days.
> 
> The incident occurred during a soccer tournament and community activities organized by residents of the area to celebrate the 205th anniversary of Guayaquil's independence. More than 150 people were gathered in a festive and joyful atmosphere when a group of hooded gunmen stormed the site in two cars and a three-wheeled motorcycle, unleashing a hail of bullets. According to preliminary data from the National Police, the attackers fired at least 85 shots, using pistols and rifles, leaving 47 9mm bullet fragments and 38 rifle casings at the scene. Panicked neighbors rushed the victims to city hospitals, while specialized police teams cordoned off the area to investigate.
> 
> The leading hypothesis points to a territorial dispute between criminal gangs operating in this vulnerable area of ??southwest Guayaquil, where the absence of the state and the advance of organized crime have turned entire neighborhoods into high-risk zones. Only two of the deceased have been identified so far, and neither had a criminal record, which underscores how indiscriminate violence primarily affects innocent people: families, children, and young people who were simply seeking a moment of peace amidst the crisis.
> 
> Kika: A trans life cut short amidst the celebration
> 
> Among the victims of this massacre was Kika, a 40-year-old trans woman, originally from the area and well-known in the LGBTIQ+ community of Guayaquil. Kika was no stranger to these activities; on the contrary, she was an active member of the soccer championship, fostering spaces of inclusion and solidarity in a neighborhood marked by marginalization and discrimination. Her presence there was not accidental: like many trans women in Guayaquil, Kika fought daily against stigmatization, poverty, and structural violence, while building support networks for her peers.
> 
> Kika's murder is not an isolated incident, but a cruel reflection of the intersection between organized crime and the endemic transphobia that permeates our society.
> 
> According to the Runa Sipiy 2025 report by Silueta X—which monitors violence against the LGBTIQ+ community—Ecuador has registered at least 15 violent deaths of trans and LGBTIQ+ people so far this year, many of them linked to extortion, protection rackets, and settling of scores in areas controlled by gangs. Kika is added to this tragic list, reminding us that trans women are the most vulnerable: in 82% of these cases, the victims had no connection to crime, but were easy targets for violence that preys on our vulnerability.
> 
> Kika's death is a devastating blow to the soul of the entire trans community, which already lives under the yoke of discrimination, unemployment, and a lack of state protection. How many more Kikas must we lose before the state recognizes transfeminicide as a specific crime and acts urgently?



> TRAGEDY IN ECUADOR: SHOOTING AT A FOOTBALL MATCH LEAVES 3 DEAD
> 
> A friendly match ended in tragedy when armed criminals stormed the pitch and opened fire. The attack left three dead and multiple wounded.
> 
> The incident occurred in Ecuador, in the province of Guayas, amidst a context of escalating violence linked to organized crime. The possibility of a settling of scores has not been ruled out.
> 
> A video of the incident shows the game abruptly stopping and the players throwing themselves to the ground as the attackers approached.
> 
> Minister John Reimberg reported that the assailants breached security controls, assaulted a guard, and entered the stadium in several vehicles, some wearing military-style clothing.
