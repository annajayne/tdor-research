> Outrage in Antioquia over the murder of a trans woman; the Ombudsman spoke out: “They shouldn’t have to live in fear.”
> The victim was identified as Yuliza Orozco. With this crime, the number of LGBTQ+ people murdered in Colombia so far in 2025 has reached 67.
> December 31, 2025 9:08 pm EST
> 
> Violence against the diverse population in Colombia persists and continues to bring grief to the nation. On December 30, 2025, Yulitza Orozco, a trans woman and member of the LGBT+ community, was found dead with multiple gunshot wounds to the head on the road connecting El Retiro and Pantanillo, in the municipality of La Ceja, in eastern Antioquia.
> 
> Her family had reported her disappearance to the authorities, and hours later her body was found with violent injurie. In addition to her loved ones, the community of La Ceja is devastated by the events, given that the victim was well-known in the area.
> 
> The organization Caribe Afirmativo presented figures from its Human Rights Observatory and warned that so far in 2025, 67 people from the LGBT+ community have been murdered in Colombia, including Yulitza Orozco. In a message published on X, the group demanded that the Prosecutor's Office conduct the investigation with a gender perspective and expressed its condolences to Orozco's family and friends.
> 
> “ We deeply regret the murder of Yulitza, a trans woman, in La Ceja, Antioquia . We call on the Colombian Attorney General's Office (@FiscaliaCol) to investigate this crime with a gender perspective. We extend our condolences to her friends and family,” the organization stated on social media.
> 
> For her part, the Ombudsman, Iris Marín, condemned the murder of the trans woman and attributed the events to a case of discrimination that escalated into the worst possible scenario of violence. Like Caribe Afirmativo, she expressed her condolences to the victim's family.
> 
> “ The murder of trans women is one of the crimes that demonstrates the levels of discrimination that should be intolerable in a democratic society . A heartfelt embrace of solidarity with the loved ones of Yulitza Orozco and all LGBTQ+ people. They should not have to live in fear and vulnerability,” the official stated.
> 
> According to Caracol Radio , the judicial investigation into the case is focused on determining whether the murder of Yulitza Orozco is directly related to her sexual orientation and gender identity or if it was due to another situation.
> 
> Another case that shocked Antioquia: the murder of Sara Millerey
> In April 2025, the people of Antioquia and the entire country mourned and condemned the murder of Sara Millerey González Borja, a trans woman who was assaulted by several men in Bello, Antioquia. She was found severely injured in a river and later died; the images caused outrage and dismay among the public and prompted authorities to act swiftly.
> 
> After several months of investigation, the Colombian Attorney General's Office formally charged two men in Medellín before a specialized judge . They are Juan Camilo Muñoz Gaviria, alias Teta or Teté, and Juan David Echavarría Zapata, alias Chuky, accused of aggravated homicide and torture.
> 
> According to the investigation conducted by the prosecution, the accused men, along with three other accomplices, detained the victim and took her to a house where they assaulted her with a stick and a rod . As a result of these serious attacks, Sara Millerey suffered fractures to her upper and lower extremities and injuries to her abdomen, chest, and lungs. Afterward, they threw her into the La García ravine.
> 
> During the attack, the alleged assailants intimidated neighbors to prevent them from intervening. Hours later, family members and members of the official fire department rescued the victim from the river and took him to a health center in Medellín, where he died the following day due to the severity of his injuries.
