> Man kills and takes the body of a young transgender woman to the police station and is released by the police in western Bahia.
> The suspect turned himself in with the victim's body, was questioned, released, and will face charges while free. The case has generated outrage and mobilized family members, organizations, and parliamentarians.
> December 9, 2025
> LGBTQIA+ Communities , Genocide of the Black Population
> 
> 
> After strangling an 18-year-old trans girl to death, an app driver who showed up at the police station with the victim's body in the car was questioned and released on Saturday night (06). The victim was Rhianna Alves. The two left the municipality of Barreiras (BA) heading towards Luís Eduardo Magalhães (BA).
> 
> According to the suspect's testimony, published by the Correio newspaper, the aggressor allegedly put the young woman in a chokehold after an argument, claiming to have acted in self-defense. The transfemicide is under investigation by the Luís Eduardo Magalhães Territorial Police Station. According to information from the Civil Police, the 18-year-old suspect remains free after voluntarily presenting himself at the police station and confessing to the crime.
> 
> On social media, Drycka Santana, the young woman's sister, expressed her grief over her sister's death. “She had a bright future ahead of her, but she didn't listen to our advice. I always repeated the same phrase: 'Get away from those friendships, they will bring you down,' and it really did. Almost a month after that fight, someone comes along and simply kills her. May all those responsible pay, because I'm certain it wasn't just one person.”
> 
> The young woman was buried on Tuesday morning (9). Desperate, Rhianna's sister is asking for justice.
> 
> What if it were the other way around?
> Congresswoman Erika Hilton spoke out on social media regarding the young woman's death. The parliamentarian stated that she reported the police chief and the perpetrator of the crime to the State Public Prosecutor's Office. Hilton demanded clarification from the Government of the State of Bahia regarding the case and officially notified the Civil Police and the Public Security Secretariat.
> 
> "It's inconceivable that a police officer wouldn't arrest a murderer who brought a body to a police station simply because he was 'good,' confessed to the crime, and pinky swore he would behave. If someone killed a coworker, a neighbor, an elderly person, or a child, brought the body to the police station, and confessed, would they be released?"
> 
> Organizations such as Fonatrans, the Permanent Commission on Sexual and Gender Diversity of the OAB Bahia (Brazilian Bar Association, Bahia chapter), and the Mothers for Resistance Collective published a joint statement, beginning with a call for reflection: What if it were the other way around?
> 
> "If a transvestite or a trans woman had killed a cisgender heterosexual man claiming self-defense, would she be released immediately in the same way!? If the state claimed there was no flagrant offense, would it accept the narrative with the same ease!? The unequal treatment is cruelly apparent. Cisgender bodies receive instant credibility and protection."
> 
> Rhianna's femicide adds to the growing number of hate crimes targeting cis and trans women in Brazil. 
> 
> Data from the Ministry of Women, released by Agência Brasil, indicates that, in 2025, Brazil will have already registered more than 1,180 femicides, which equates to an average of about three femicides per day. In addition, nearly 3,000 daily calls were recorded to the 180 helpline. In the previous year, the Annual Socioeconomic Report on Women (Raseam) recorded 1,450 femicides in the country and 2,485 intentional homicides and bodily injuries resulting in death.




> LUÍS EDUARDO MAGALHÃES
> 'Mata-leão': find out who the ride-hailing driver is who used the chokehold to kill a young transgender woman.
> The author is responding to the charges while free.
> Published on December 9, 2025 at 4:46 PM
> 
> Sérgio Henrique Lima dos Santos  is the name of the app driver who strangled to death  the young trans woman Rhianna, 18 , in the city of Luís Eduardo Magalhães, in the west of the state, last Saturday (6). Sérgio, who is 19 years old, presented himself to the police station on the same day, admitted to having applied a “mata-leão” after an alleged argument and is responding in freedom for the crime of femicide. 
> 
> Rhianna, who lived in Barreiras, was strangled inside the vehicle. In his testimony, Sérgio stated that he had hired the victim for a sexual encounter and that, after the meeting, he was taking her back home when the argument began, at which point the victim allegedly threatened to expose the contracted service. However, the version regarding prostitution is disputed.
> 
> “Rhianna worked at a supermarket in Luís Eduardo. I spoke with other trans women who work as sex workers, and none of them recognized Rhianna as a sex worker. Nothing against the work, but associating her with that is a way of reinforcing a stigma against trans women,” declares Melyssa Chaves, a member of Mães da Resistência (Mothers of Resistance), an organization dedicated to supporting mothers, fathers, and family members of LGBTQIAPN+ people, and which is following the case.
> 
> See who the young woman killed with a chokehold by an app driver is.
> 
> Another point raised is that Sérgio and Rhianna had met before. "They had a long-standing relationship," says a resident. The victim began the transition process about five months ago, shortly after turning 18.
> 
> Freedom
> 
> The ride-share driver was questioned and released by the police chief of Luís Eduardo Magalhães, Joaquim Rodrigues. The release caused a stir in the city and on social media, becoming the target of a complaint from congresswoman Érika Hilton. On one of her profiles, the congresswoman stated that she had denounced the police chief responsible for the driver's release. “I am denouncing the murderer and the police chief to the State Public Prosecutor's Office. [...] It is inconceivable that a police chief would not arrest a murderer who brought a body to a police station because he was 'nice,' confessed to the crime, and pinky swore he would behave,” she wrote.
> 
> The congresswoman also said that she sent an official letter to the Civil Police, the Public Security Secretariat, and the Government of Bahia demanding clarification.
> 
> Contacted for comment, the Civil Police of Bahia (PCBA) reported that they have opened an investigation and that, because the suspect presented himself voluntarily and confessed to the crime, he was released and will face charges while free.
> 
> Rhianna's sister opened up on social media.
> 
> What if it were the other way around?
> 
> Eronildo Pereira de Queiroz, a criminal lawyer and resident of Luís Eduardo, explains that  Brazilian law stipulates that a confession or voluntary appearance does not obligate the police to keep the suspect in custody . According to him, preventive detention requires the absence of certain prerequisites, such as being a first-time offender, having a fixed address, and voluntarily appearing in court.
> 
> “But during the investigation, nothing prevents the police chief from requesting the arrest of the suspect, for example, if the expert reports contradict the version presented by him or if there is contact or threats to witnesses,” explains Queiroz.
> 
> However, Melyssa Chaves reflects: “If the victim were a cisgender woman (one who was born with female genitalia and identifies as a woman), would the perpetrator have been released? Or if the perpetrator were a trans woman who killed a man in self-defense against daily aggression? Would there be such understanding that she could be released on bail?”, she points out.
> 
> 10 cases
> 
> According to the NGO Mothers of Resistance, at least 10 LGBT people have been murdered in the last 10 years in the city of Luís Eduardo Magalhães. The  trans woman Lorena Fox was found dead on March 1, 2023. The death certificate lists the cause of death as "trauma and skull fracture." In July 2020, Guilherme de Souza, just 21 years old, was assaulted, stoned, and burned alive.
> 
> “LGBTphobia is intricately linked to sexism, the far-right, and religious fundamentalism. It is no coincidence that, for the past 15 years, Brazil has led in the number of deaths of LGBT people,” declares Melyssa Chaves.
