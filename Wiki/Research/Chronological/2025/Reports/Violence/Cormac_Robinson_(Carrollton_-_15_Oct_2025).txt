> Cormac Robinson was murdered by his (Ex?)partner (who then killed himself) on October 15, 2025 in Carrollton, GA. They were deadnamed and misgendered in all news reports and by their unsupportive family.



> (CW: death, transphobia, and gun violence)
> Yesterday was Transgender Day of Rememberance. This year hit really hard because on October 15th, my close friend Cormac was shot and killed.
> 
> There's a number that was floating around on my timeline yesterday. 27. There have been 27 recorded trans people who died this year due to hate crimes. That doesn't include all of them, only the ones who were recorded that way. It doesn't include trans people who were killed,  but had unsupportive families so their death was never even investigated as a hate crime, or those who were killed for being trans, yet their death wasn't prosecuted as a hate crime. We will never have that true number.
> 
> Cormac was shot and killed in the back while sitting at his computer playing video games by his boyfriend who he was planning to leave at the time. Part of why he was planning to leave was because his boyfriend was trying to convince him to detransition according to a lot of his friends. We will never know why he was shot because the shooter then shot himself afterwards.
> 
> Cormac's family wasn't supportive of his transition which lead him to moving across the country, away from Florida, several times. When we met back in 2020, he was living in Kentucky and attending an Indiana Convention. He was living in Georgia when he died. Since his family was legally in charge of everything after his death, all reports, news articles,  and even his obituary called him a woman, and by a name that wasn't his for over a decade. They refused to use recent pictures of him despite having access to hundreds of them, so the picture for his obituary is from 7 years ago when he was in high school before he transitioned. It didn't matter that he came out over a decade ago, that he had been on T for 5 years or got top surgery two years ago. They didn't care about who he was or about remembering him as how he lived, they only cared about who they wanted him to be. 
> 
> This is why Transgender Day of Rememberance is so important to me, so many of us won't be remembered as who we are, but instead as what people wish we were. It's a cruel reality of being trans, which makes it vital for us to tell the stories of those who died so that their truth can live on.
> 
> Cormac was creative, kind, funny,  careing, and so much more. He loved animals,  especially bunnies, he loved to cosplay,  watch Anime, draw, and so much more. He was such a light in this world. He had so many friends,  he called all of them his melons (except for me, he jokingly called me his kiwi since I'm allergic to melon). All of us will do our best to remember him for who he was. His discord server has been left to me, and I am doing my best to keep it alive for him. We all love and miss him so much. Server calls aren't the same without him cracking a joke every 5 minutes or doing an interesting craft in the background. I miss calling him when 
> 
> I was bored or needed someone to talk to while doing chores. I will always remember the light he held in my life.
> Feel free to share this post as a reminder that there have been far more than just 27 trans people killed this year.
> 
> I want to conclude this with a poem I wrote years ago that feels very relevant right now.
>

> Two die in apparent murder-suicide
> By DOUGLAS SALTER dsalter@times-georgian.com Oct 23, 2025 Updated Oct 23, 2025 
> Two Carrollton residents were found dead Oct. 15 in an apparent murder-suicide, according to Carrollton police.
> 
> Authorities said the bodies of 21-year-old Richard Austin Garner and 24-year-old Penyha “Cormac” Annette Robinson were discovered inside their home at River Pointe Apartments. The police report indicated Garner killed Robinson and then died by suicide.
> 
> The couple was discovered after officers initiated a welfare check at the request of a family member, who reportedly was concerned that Garner had not been heard from since a he sent a message through Facebook Messenger at around 8 p.m. on Oct. 13.
> 
> The family member told police that Garner — who served in the U.S. Army and was was classified as 80% disabled by the VA — had been off his prescribed anxiety and depression medication for about two months, and that he “attempted suicide by motorcycle accident” in 2023, the report stated.
> 
> Officers arrived at the Lovvorn Road complex and waited outside the apartment at the request of the property manager, who unlocked the door so a maintenance employee could evaluate the scene, according to the police report. There was no indication of a break-in, the CPD said.
> 
> Once inside, the employee advised the officers to enter the apartment, where they reportedly found the bodies of Garner and Robinson in a bedroom.
> 
> It was unknown at the time of the deaths whether either had filed previous complaints regarding alleged abuse.
