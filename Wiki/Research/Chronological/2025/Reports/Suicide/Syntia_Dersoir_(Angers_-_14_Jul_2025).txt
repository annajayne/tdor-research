> Her name was Syntia Dersoir, born August 24, 2002, and who died by suicide at the age of 22 on July 14, 2025. 




> Segré. The McDonald's in Segré is being sued in labor court: "They forbade me from using my new first name."
> 
> Syntia Dersoir is a transgender woman. She was employed in 2022 by the McDonald's in Segré under her male identity before officially transitioning. For several months, she has been in the labor court against her employer, whom she is suing for transphobia.
> 
> Syntia Dersoir, a young transgender woman from Segré, has taken her case to the labor court because she accuses her employer of transphobia.
> 
> Discreetly, she arrives at the café table in Segré where she tells her story. Syntia Dersoir is a transgender woman, originally from the town. Upon her return from a year of studies in La Rochelle: "I needed a job, and this was the only one that responded. It was going well until... well, that's it."
> 
> "There you have it "  : a complaint filed with the labor court against her employer, the McDonald's in Segré, and another with the police for " harassment and discrimination ." The young woman says she was a victim of transphobia by her employer and several managers. The first hearing, scheduled for April 15 in Angers, was adjourned until June 24. Syntia does not yet have a date for the second hearing.
> 
> Syntia Dersoir started working at McDonald's in September 2022. She was hired there under her male name before she began her gender transition. "I announced my trans identity on January 9, 2023, but apparently everyone already knew. A colleague had seen me dressed as a girl and wearing makeup in town and had mentioned it."
> 
> On the day she came out, Syntia wore makeup  : "The manager didn't say anything, nor did the customers, they didn't care."She recounts that, two weeks later, a supervisor summoned her for a warning about her makeup, as she mentioned in an email to the labor inspectorate. In the company, job titles are listed on a whiteboard. "The next day, I decided to erase my first name and replace it with the new one ," Syntia states. " They changed the name back. They forbade the use of my new name.  "
> 
> She claims that her manager then approached her to explain that he had been reprimanded by his superiors because, according to management, changing a first name is forbidden. For McDonald's, the situation is unprecedented.
> 
> This is the first time the company has found itself in labor court for this type of accusation.
> 
> Syntia claims that "the documents from the Defender of Rights clearly state that, even if it hasn't been officially changed, they have the right to modify it." However, the Defender of Rights' decision only recommends this position to employers. Indeed, the company does not have the right to change the employee's legal identity on official documents such as payslips or the single personnel register. The same rule must apply to employers to ensure that the gender recorded on the birth certificate is respected in all their legal obligations. Thus, an employer may decide, in the interest of inclusivity, to accept an employee's change of first name, but this obliges them to manage both the employee's legal identity and their new identity according to the specific circumstances and documents.
> 
> The young woman also denounces a series of remarks, summonses, refusals to work, and warnings." A manager told me: ' We understand that you want to become a girl, but you don't have to wear so much makeup.'" She also says she heard  : "Today, we call him by a woman's name, but he's not a woman, he's a man."
> 
> On February 13, Syntia was reportedly summoned to a superior's office because her makeup wasn't suitable.  "She told me she couldn't allow me to work in the field, that I had to remove some lipstick."   Syntia then went to the restroom to remove it. "It still wasn't right. She told me that if I wanted, I could go to the Super U supermarket to buy makeup remover and come back, or else I could go home. So I went home." The next day, another of her superiors allegedly told her  : "You impose your choices on us, you only think about yourself, you're putting us in a terrible position."
> 
> Around that time, Syntia says she started keeping her phone's microphone on. "A manager forbade a colleague from calling me Syntia. My colleague asked why, and they explained that we were being paid ."
> 
> Today, the young woman hopes for the judicial termination of her contract.Having worked for less than a year, Syntia received daily allowances for only six months. " I had to stop laser hair removal... I have no more money. I owe €150 in medical fees. I can't even call them, I don't have a number anymore. I'm on leave so it's all going backwards, it pisses me off... I'm dependent on my mother."
