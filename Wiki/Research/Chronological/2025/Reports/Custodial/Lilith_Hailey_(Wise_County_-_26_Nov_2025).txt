> SETTLEMENT REACHED
> Chesterfield family set to receive $2.7 million after, a transgender woman who went by the name Lilith, family member died at Red Onion State Prison. 
> ERIC KOLENICH Richmond Times-Dispatch 
> 
> The Chesterfield County family of a prison inmate who died last year is set to receive $2.7 million in a wrongful death settlement, according to a court filing made last week.
> 
> Michael Hailey died in November 2025 at Red Onion State Prison in Wise County. The state health department ruled the death a homicide.
> 
> A judge is scheduled to rule on the proposed settlement next month. 
> 
> Hailey was one 105 people to die at a Virginia state prison last year, and one of two whose death was ruled a homicide. The other, Aubrey McKay, died in June 2025 at Wallens Ridge State Prison also in Wise County. 
> 
> The Virginia Department of Health determined the cause of Hailey's death was asphyxia due to chest compression, news outlets reported. According to a prisoners' family group, Hailey was a transgender woman who went by the name Lilith. The group claims that during a lockdown, correctional officers fired less-lethal rounds at Hailey, used a taser and chemical irritants, then an officer sat on Hailey's chest.
> 
> No charges have been announced in connection to Hailey's death. The Virginia Department of Corrections has investigated the matter and referred the completed investigation to Brett Hall, the top prosecutor for Wise County and the city of Norton to make a decision, a spokesperson for the department said.
> 
> Hall's office did not respond to a request for comment. 
> 
> Michael Marcotte, Hailey's father, is set to receive roughly $986,000 from the Department of Corrections. Hailey's brother, Nicholaus Marcotte, would receive $815,000, and the family's law firm, the Richmond-based Krudys Law firm, would get $884,000, according to court records. The plaintiffs have asked the court to approve the settlement. The law firm did not respond to a request for comment. 
> 
> In 2024, at least six inmates at Red Onion, the state's only maximum-security prison, burned themselves using "improvised devices" made by altering electrical outlets, Chad Dotson, then-chief of prisons, said at the time. Sen. Mike Jones, D-Richmond, conducted an unannounced visit and said he received numerous complaints about racism by corrections officers and poor medical treatment at the jail. 
> 
> In January, Gov. Abigail Spanberger appointed Joseph Walters to lead the Department of Corrections, replacing Dotson.
