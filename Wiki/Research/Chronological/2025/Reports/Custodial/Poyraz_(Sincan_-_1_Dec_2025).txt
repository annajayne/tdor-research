> EMEP's Karaca brought the suspicious death of a transgender prisoner in Sincan Prison to the Parliament's agenda: Allegations of 4 deaths in 4 months.
> 
> December 12, 2025 11:33
> 
> -A+
> T24 Video
> 
> EMEP Gaziantep MP Sevda Karaca brought the death of Poyraz, a transgender male prisoner, who was found dead in his cell the same day he was transferred to Sincan Prison, to the attention of the Parliament, and raised allegations that four deaths had occurred in the same prison in four months.
> 
> Sevda Karaca, in a press conference she held in Parliament yesterday, brought up the suspicious death of a transgender male prisoner named Poyraz in Sincan Women's Closed Prison.
> 
> In her statement, Karaca included information she obtained from her meeting with female lawyers from the Ankara Bar Association, stating that transgender and LGBT+ prisoners were forcibly placed in G-3 cell, which is called the "groom's ward," and said the following:
> 
> “They mockingly call this cell the ‘groom’s ward,’ where they forcibly place transgender and LGBTQ+ prisoners, making them sign dubious papers. They isolate these cells, selecting those female prisoners who are ‘short-haired’ or ‘masculine.’ They are kept in solitary confinement, preventing contact with anyone, even during doctor visits, and not allowing them to have any contact with others, even during fresh air. They are denied the right to exercise. During their time here, those undergoing gender transition are subjected to various forms of torture to discourage them from seeking treatment.”
> 
> "Prisoners are suddenly being taken to psychiatric hospitals en masse."
> Karaca emphasized that the practices were not limited to these, stating that four more prisoners were recently placed in this cell and taken to a psychiatrist collectively. Karaca continued:
> 
> “On December 1st, these prisoners were suddenly taken to the psychiatric ward en masse, in a manner never before seen. They were kept there for about two hours. When they returned to their cells, they found a transgender man named Poyraz, who had never been in that ward before, hanging from the bunk bed. The prisoners said that Poyraz's hair had been shaved off and that he looked much thinner than usual. They pressed the emergency button and called for help. One of the prisoners tried to cut the rope with a lighter because there were no cutting tools available. Guards went in and out of the ward, but no medical personnel arrived. Later, the guards wrapped Poyraz in a blanket and carried him out. A few days later, they were informed that he had died.”
> 
> "They are being held responsible for the death of a person they had never stayed with before, and whom they found hanging when they returned to the room."
> Karaca stated that two days after the incident, the prosecutor and the crime scene investigation team came to the prison, but the statements of the prisoners in the ward were not taken, and added the following:
> 
> “When the prosecutor arrives, the prisoners in the ward are locked in another room. They say they want to give statements to the prosecutor, but the prosecutor doesn't take them. Later, a disciplinary investigation is launched against the prisoners in that ward regarding this incident. You heard that right. They are being held responsible for the death of a person they had never shared a cell with before, who was placed in the room when they were taken to the doctor, and whom they found hanging when they returned.”




> 10/12/2025 |Author: Kaos GL
> According to lawyers who visited the prisoners, the medical team took a long time to arrive at the scene. The rope the prisoner was hanging from was not in the room before.
> A transgender male prisoner was found dead under suspicious circumstances in Sincan Prison. Kaos GL - LGBTI+ News Portal
> 
> Human rights violations against transgender prisoners are once again in the spotlight. According to Eylem Esen Arabaci of Pembe Hayat, a transgender male prisoner named Poyraz was found dead in his cell at Sincan Prison. Following his death, the prosecutor's office arrived at the prison two days later for an investigation; however, witness statements were not taken.
> 
> Following the suspicious death, serious human rights violations regarding prison conditions also came to light.
> 
> Lawyers who visited the prisoners shared with KaosGL.org the accounts of transgender prisoners who have been held in solitary confinement for a long time.
> 
> The medical team took a long time to arrive.
> According to information provided by lawyers, four transgender male prisoners in Sincan Prison have been held in solitary confinement for a long time. It is stated that on the day the four prisoners were taken to the psychiatric ward together, a new person was transferred to their cell; when the prisoners returned, they found this person hanged.
> 
> According to the prisoners, they initially tried to administer first aid themselves by cutting the rope with a lighter. It was reported that chaos ensued in the room after the guards were called, but the medical team took a long time to arrive.
> 
> The rope by which the prisoner was hanged was not in the room.
> The prisoners stated that the person's pulse was very weak at first, that their hair had been shaved off, and that it was unclear how a rope, which was not present in the room, had been brought in. According to the statements of the lawyers who visited the prisoners, some guards reportedly said, "They already had problems," after the incident.
> 
> The lawyers summarized what the prisoners told them as follows:
> 
> “According to the transgender prisoners' accounts, after the incident, guards were called, a commotion ensued in the room, and the medical team never arrived. Initially, they said the prisoner's pulse was very weak and they looked very ill with their hair shaved off. The rope wasn't in the room; it's unknown how it was brought in. The incident doesn't appear to be a suicide. The rope wasn't in the room; the newly arrived prisoner had their hair shaved off, and four transgender prisoners were taken to a psychiatrist together for the first time.”
> 
> The director, who came from Bakirköy, initiated discriminatory treatment against transgender prisoners.
> Lawyers stated that the director, who started his job at the prison a few months ago and came from Bakirköy, has initiated discriminatory practices against transgender prisoners, noting that prisoners are frequently asked questions such as "How do you feel?", "What is your orientation?", and that those who say "I am not heterosexual" are treated differently.
> 
> Even having short hair has become grounds for being sent to separate wards.
> Even having short hair has become grounds for transferring prisoners to separate cells, and it has been reported that some prisoners have completely abandoned the gender transition process for this reason.
> 
> “Even having short hair has become a reason for segregating prisoners. Trans people in other wards have given up on their own paths because of this.”
> 
> Trans prisoners were forced to sign a document without being allowed to read it, and one prisoner attempted suicide.
> Lawyers reported that one of the four transgender prisoners recently attempted suicide. According to the lawyers, the prisoners also stated that they were forced to sign a document without being allowed to read it, which reportedly stated, "I wish to be discriminated against because of my gender identity."
> 
> “Prisoners say they were forced to sign a document without reading it, a document stating, ‘I want to be discriminated against because of my gender identity.’ Those who sign this document are being discriminated against. Access to hormone therapy and hospital care is being blocked.”
