> A tragedy that could have been avoided: Young Cuban trans woman dies after industrial silicone injection
> Popó, as the young woman from Matanzas was known, died on December 18, after spending 11 days in the Faustino Pérez Hospital, due to a pulmonary embolism and cardiac arrest caused by the injected substance, the family revealed in statements to CiberCuba.
> Monday, December 22, 2025 - 8:15 PM
> 
> The recent death of a young Cuban trans woman, after being injected with industrial silicone in a clandestine cosmetic procedure in Havana, has shocked her family, who have denounced the case and are demanding justice to prevent anyone else from dying.
> 
> Popó, as the young woman from Matanzas was known, died on December 18, after spending 11 days in the Faustino Pérez Hospital, due to a pulmonary embolism and cardiac arrest caused by the injected substance, relatives told CiberCuba.
> 
> According to the testimony of a cousin - whose name we will keep confidential - Popó bought an industrial product identified as liquid silicone in the capital and contacted a person to inject it into his buttocks.
> 
> He explained that the person who performed the procedure has not yet been identified, and they are asking the authorities for an investigation to find the person responsible, in order to prevent other people from also losing their lives in cosmetic surgeries without sanitary control.
> 
> "An industrial product called liquid silicone was injected into the buttocks; that happened in Havana and nobody is giving us answers, we don't have a blame (...) We don't know who it is," he stressed.
> 
> The family sent CiberCuba photos of the one-liter bottle of Silicone 350 CP—manufactured for industrial, not medical, use—that was used in the procedure. The label on the bottle indicates that it is used for technical lubrication, as an antifoaming agent in electrical and electronic equipment, as an additive for plastics, and for polishing, but never for human use. The manufacturer warns that “in case of exposure,” medical assistance is required.
> 
> “That product is being sold in Havana for clandestine cosmetic surgeries,” Popó's cousin warned.
> 
> The woman described the terrible effects the substance had on the young woman's body, which led to her death. "This liquid caused a blood clot in her lung (...), around the fourth day she arrived in Matanzas, at the Faustino Pérez Hospital, with a lung complication, coughing up blood, very short of breath, and was admitted to intermediate care. She was there for nine days, until she had almost a pulmonary hemorrhage and was transferred to intensive care, where she stayed for two days. She was hospitalized for 11 days. Her lungs stopped functioning and finally her heart," she lamented.
> 
> “His family demands justice, we want to know who did this so that other lives are not at risk, please help me,” she pleaded. 
> 
> “We want justice because we know that this procedure is still being carried out in Havana on trans girls, and we don't want any more to die,” said the relative of the deceased young woman.
>

> Popó's death has caused deep consternation in the Cuban LGBTI community, particularly among trans people, who join his family in demanding justice.

But she was killed by the person who performed the cosmetic procedure (...) it was the fault of the one who did it. Justice," demanded Mónica King, an activist for the rights of the LGBTI community.
